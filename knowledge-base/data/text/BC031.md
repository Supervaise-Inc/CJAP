# Love God, Serve Man -- Ch. 17: A Plea for Honesty
Date:       1988-11-17
Publisher:  Philippine Daily Inquirer (ed. Isagani Yambot)
Source:     Love God, Serve Man, Philippine Daily Inquirer (ed. Isagani Yambot) — ch. 17
By: Artemio V. Panganiban

(Editor's note: As a student, Attorney Panganiban distinguished himself not only as a university scholar but also as a topnotch student leader. He was probably the best known campus leader of his time, having been the youngest president of the Far Eastern University Central Student Organization while he was still a second year law student, a record that has never been broken since 1957. In 1958, he founded and headed the National Union of Students of the Philippines [NUSP]. Up to the present, the NUSP is the largest and most representative student movement in the country. On November 17, 1988, the author was invited to keynote the National Student Congress of NUSP in Cebu City. His speech follows.)

May I thank you most sincerely, especially the charismatic incumbent NUSP President Lorenzo Tanada III, for honoring me today as your keynote speaker during the National Student Congress sponsored by the National Union of Students of the Philippines (NUSP) here in Cebu City, the Queen City of the South. This occasion brings back cherished memories of my own student days 30 years ago - of my own search for identity, my singleminded pursuit of excellence and my own formation of enduring values. I treasure these memories even as I recall some of the happiest and most enjoyable years of my life when I could unabashedly say I was truly free. I was very poor; I had nothing. I protected no worldly possessions and interests. I pursued only my youthful yearnings and ideas of what was true, good and beautiful.

In a sense, I was fortunate to have been only a second year law student when in 1957, I was elected president of the FEU Central Student Organization and subsequently organized and headed the National Union of Students of the Philippines, which was then and still now, the largest, most representative and most prestigious student association in the country. Because of this, I was

able to devote three years of my student life, from 1957 to 1960, when I graduated from the College of Law, in organizing, molding and strengthening NUSP during its formative years. Thereafter, from 1960 to 1962, I organized the Institute of Student Affairs, later known as World University Service, Philippines (or WUS Philippines), which continued to minister and assist Philippine student movements with material resources and physical needs. It was in the late '50s when as a young upstart I met such dedicated young men of destiny like Emanuel Soriano, Fernando Lagua, Alfonso Aguirre, Homobono Adaza, Leonardo Tancuan, Salvador Malbarosa and John Osmeiia, who helped in organizing NUSP. And it was in the early '60s, when as a young lawyer, I met talented, up and coming men, like Raul Roco, Rene Saguisag, Jesus Elbinas, Raul Paredes, Oscar Contreras, and Evergisto Macatulad, who helped carry the torch of NUSP leadership.

In the rolls of its alumni-leaders through the late ' 60s, '70s' and '80s, are names like Ricardo Puno Jr., Michael Mastura, Salvador Britanico, Violeta Calvo-Drilon, Elfren Cruz, Jose Cuisia, Macapanton Abbas Jr., Carlos Padilla, Mervyn Encanto, Herman Laurel, Miriam Defensor-Santiago, Tina Monzon-Palma, Jose Lina, Sonny Ramirez, Brigido Simon, Edgar Jopson, Francis Pangilinan, Lean Alejandro and Chito Gascon.

The NUSP was born as a rebellion against a decadent student milieu that was prostituted and muzzled by the government then. The NUSP was formed out of the vision that the young must be freed from the political apron strings of the old. The NUSP espoused the idea and ideal that the student can think for himself, knows what is best for him and for his future, and thus must be given the freedom to express himself and to pursue his destiny. The NUSP was founded on the proposition that the best in man and in society can be attained only in freedom, dignity, equality and responsibility.

The founders of NUSP pursued these ideals during our time. Thirty years later, the NUSP is alive and well, It has grown

and matured, survived and met the challenge of time. Now, victorious and triumphant, it leads the young not only in secular concerns of education and studying, but in the battles of our country and people to find real political independence, economic revival and social upliftment. I believe I can speak for my co-founders when I say to all of you attending this Student Congress: "Well done! Well done!"

In fact, I venture to say that throughout the years, the NUSP has accomplished more than what we, its founders, dreamt it could do. During our time, the NUSP's activities were largely secular. They were limited to the concerns of students as students — that is, how to improve educational standards, how to prepare ourselves to become better citizens, how to enlarge our educational outlook through non-academic pursuits, and the like. • But throughout the years, NUSP's activities transcended those of the student and graduated to those of the citizen. Thus, it spearheaded the battles against corruption, dictatorship, imperialism and terrorism. And true to this tradition, the current NUSP's concerns are not limited to the confines of academic walls but overflow into the raging war against poverty, colonialism, oppression and decadence.

Indeed, the student movement in the Philippines has come of age. It is concerned not only with itself, but with the nation. It is concerned not only with the present, but with the future as well. And this is as it should be, for to the young really belongs this Pearl of the Orient country of ours.

This keynote address is not merely a message of congratulations for what NUSP has done during the last 30 years; it is equally a vision of what NUSP could do during the next 30. It is true that the political dictatorship has been toppled, that domestic institutions, like Congress, have been put in place and that the immediate dangers of violent coups and take-overs have passed. But this does not mean that our problems are over. On the contrary, there are still enormous challenges facing our people and our country. I realize that the present NUSP leadership has taken firm positions with regard to many of these problems.

Let me, however, put forth just one more question, one more thought, one more concern in your effort at nation-building: Why is it that amongst the countries and peoples in this part of the Pacific, the Philippines and the Filipinos are the only country and people that have remained poor and deprived? True, our oppression has been lessened but our poverty remains. Those of you who have traveled to South Korea, Taiwan, Malaysia, Thailand, not to say Japan, Hong Kong and Singapore, must have marvelled at the tremendous socio-economic strides these countries have undergone during the last decade. Is it because there were no dictators in these countries? Of course not! Without meaning to mitigate the guilt of the man in Hawaii, I daresay that Korea, Taiwan, even Singapore and some other Asian countries have had their share of dictators. Is it because foreigners did not "exploit" the natives in these countries? No, foreigners were and are still present in all these countries, but the locals have managed to fight and prevail over them. Is it because these other Asians are better educated than we? No, not at all. Percentage-wise, more Filipino finish high school and college than many of our Asian brothers. Is it because the Filipino is an inferior professional and worker? No, on the contrary, when the Filipino works abroad side by side with other nationals, he always excels. In fact, it seems the Filipino is praised everywhere, except when he is in his own country.

If the answer is not the presence of dictators, or the exploitation by foreigners, or poor education, or professional inferiority, then what? Why is the Philippines still poor in the midst of plenty in the Pacific?

One reason is that the Filipino has spoiled himself into believing and assuming that, by himself, he can do nothing; and, that to be able to accomplish something, he must depend on a patron. This "godfather mentality" manifests itself every day. If he wants a job, he thinks he must get a recommendation from an influential godfather. He does not depend on his qualifications. If he wants to win a suit in court, he thinks he must know the judge. He does not rely on the merits of his case. And if he cannot get ahead with the

use of influence, he uses chicanery. He tries to hoodwink others. He wants the easy way out. And if chicanery fails, he uses bribery. He pays his way to get what he needs and deserves. Yes, influence, chicanery and bribery have been depended upon to take the place of honest, hard work and self-discipline.

This malaise is better understood when translated into Tagalog.

Tatlong "L" po ang gamit ng Pilipino upang umunlad ang kaniyang buhay LAKAS (influence), LUSOT (chicanery) at LAGAY (bribery).

These can best be illustrated in the way my favorite taxi driver escapes punishment for his blatant traffic violations. First, he tries to sweet-talk his way (LUSOT), then he drops the name of the chief of police (LAKAS) and finally he forks out a 20-peso bill (LAGAY). In one way or the other, he merrily gets away and continues to mess up traffic every day.

These three "Ls" of solving life's problems have actually been encouraged by our culture and even by government systems. Alas, our people have been trained by our government, by the 20 years of Marcosian dictatorship, to look up to the government, or more accurately, to government administrators, for everything they need to go on with life. If the people want their garbage collected, they must lobby with their mayor; if they want their streets repaired they must see their congressman; if they want to win a government contract, they must be nice to some government bigwig. Nothing works without a lobby. Nothing moves without one having to kowtow to a government functionary. No baptism, no wedding and no town fiesta is complete without a government VIP. This is why politicians spend tremendous amounts of money, cheat brazenly and even kill in order to win elections. Because victory means not only prestige and honor, but the capability to dispense patronage, and determine the life, liberty or property of everyone around them.

Many of us, especially those who risked their lives, fortunes and families during the struggle against the dictatorship, are

deeply frustrated and we ask ourselves: Two and a half years after Marcos left, why is it that our people are still poor? Why is government still inefficient? Why is graft and corruption still pervasive in our midst? Why are the 3 "Ls" - LUSOT, LAKAS and LAGAY - still prevalent?

This is because we merely had a change of leaders, not a change in values; a change of men, not a change in men. The obsession for power, the passion for wealth, the attachment to worldly possessions, the desire for human gratification still dominate men's hearts; overrule their mission of genuine service to the national; and obfuscate compassion for the poor and the oppressed. How I wish the elder generation still had the idealism of the young, the worldly detachment of students!

But alas, our government leaders are enjoying the Marcosian habit of making themselves the ultimate source of all bounty and power, so that everyone must always beg for their grace and blessing.

Many times I have spoken before leaders of our country, before civic clubs and even religious groups denouncing this "godfather mentality" and this "doleout" syndrome that pervade our people's habits. I have assailed the three "Ls" and I have pleaded for a return to the old-fashioned virtues of honesty, hard work and self-discipline. But my plea has fallen on deaf ears.

So now, I bring this plea before the youth of our land. I ask you to help in denouncing these evil ways, these evil syndromes of LUSOT, LAKAS and LAGAY. Let us denounce those who plunder the nation, corrupt our people and deprecate our values. Let our elders learn a lesson from the young. Let your idealism, your detachment from power and wealth be your strength in resisting and fighting these evil ways.

The NUSP has helped us in crushing the dictatorship. The - NUSP excelled in the fight against colonialism and imperialism.

Now, I challenge the NUSP to lead in the crusade to have a change, not just of men, but a change in men; to spearhead the fight against the "godfather mentality" and the dole-out syndrome," and to lead in the aspiration for honesty, hard work and self-discipline.

Ibagsak ang LUSOT, LAKAS, at LAGAY. Ipugay ang KATAPATAN, KASIPAGAN at KATIMPIHAN.

Maraming salamat po.
