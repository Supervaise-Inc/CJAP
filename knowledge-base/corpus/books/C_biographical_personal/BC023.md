# Love God, Serve Man -- Ch. 1: Love God, Serve Man

Editor's note: This was the address delivered by Atty. Artemio V. Panganiban during his induction as president of the Rotary Club of Manila, the oldest and largest civic club in Asia, on July 12, 1990 at the Manila Hotel Fiesta Pavilion. On this occasion, he announced his personal credo in life, LOVE GOD, SERVE MAN, exclaiming, "Upon this credo, Love God, Serve Man, I shall be judged, not only in the Rotary Club of Manila but, I daresay, in my whole unworthy life here on earth." This speech was published in two installments in the Manila Bulletin issues of July 14 and 17, 1990.)

## Salute to a Venerable Civic Club with a Long History of Unparalleled Achievements

I thank my fellow members in the Rotary Club of Manila for installing me as president during the 72nd year of this venerable civic club. As you may know, I was never a candidate for the presidency, as in fact, no one really runs for president in Manila Rotary. He is simply elected to it. This is because under our election procedure, which is unique in the entire world of Rotary, the Rotarian who gathers the highest number of votes in the election for members of the board of directors is automatically proclaimed president, without his aspiring for it and sometimes even contrary to his wishes.

I am humbled by the thought that the presidency of Manila Rotary has a long and undisputed tradition of excellence. I realize that all my predecessors were great and honorable men, among whom were a Senate president, Gil J. Puyat; a learned justice of the Supreme Court, George A. Malcolm; and, the best-known Filipino diplomat, soldier, educator and statesman, Carlos P. Romulo. In this connection, let me pay a special tribute to the most senior living head of Manila Rotary, who was president 34 years ago - in 1956- 57- and who is still active in our Club today. I refer to businessman, banker and contractor par excellence, Jonby Carlos, whom I thank for giving me the privilege of leaning on him for support and counsel.

I realize also that the Rotary Club of Manila itself has a long history of unparalleled achievements. It is the oldest Club in Asia. With its 455 distinguished members, it is the largest outside the United States. And to quote my former professor, Supreme Court Justice Edgardo L. Paras, who is himself an outstanding member of our Club: "Manila Rotary is not only the first and largest Rotary Club in Asia. It is undoubtedly the best civic club on this side of the world."

My dear fellow members, you have indeed loaded a heavy burden upon my lean and unworthy shoulders - by catapulting me to the presidency of the premier civic club in our country.

## Program of Action, 1990-1991

Fortunately, you elected me as early as December 1989 and thus you mercifully gave me seven months to pray about, reflect on and plan for my incumbency. I have spent these seven months in continuous meetings with my incoming team, particularly my fellow directors, officers and committee chairmen and together, we have come out with a printed Program of Action, copies of which were distributed to you earlier. This program is organized along the four traditional avenues of Rotary service. Basically, however, it contains three things: ONE, it states in clear and concise language, our goals and objectives; TWO, it describes in detail the various projects and activities that we, and in particular our 67 committees, will undertake to achieve these goals; and THREE, it specifies the days and the months during our term of one year when these activities shall be undertaken and completed.

Vision of What Manila

Rotary Stands For Instead of laboriously reading to you this program of action, let me describe to you my VISION of what Manila Rotary stands for. This VISION of Manila Rotary is the basis and rationale of the program of action. It consists of three parts or points.

FIRST. Manila Rotary is for our members, for our colleagues in the other Rotary Clubs both here and abroad, and for our guests. That is why:

• We will present a well-prepared, balanced, informative and newsworthy meeting every Thursday here at Manila Hotel, where our members, colleagues and guests may enjoy and be informed of the latest events and news in our country.

• We shall continue to publish a revitalized weekly Balita, together with a new monthly supplement and semestral report of our accomplishments.

• We shall hold weekly fellowship activities in our homes and offices, as well as sponsor quarterly sports competition among members and colleagues.

• We shall pursue more aggressively our energy and water conservation programs and traffic safety activities.

• We shall increase our club membership from the present 455 to 500 and increase our average attendance percentage from the current 78 percent to 83 percent.

• We shall actively cooperate with other Rotary Clubs through our Speakers Bureau, Inter-Club Fora and Rotary Extension, as well as participate in district, national and international activities and conferences.

## Manila Rotary is for Excellence in the Professions, in Business, in Education and in Government.

SECOND. Manila Rotary is for excellence in the professions, in business, in education and in government. That is why:

• We shall actively promote, on a regular basis, the use of the Rotary 4-way test not only in our professions and businesses but also in the major city, municipal and barangay centers all over the Philippines.

• We shall strengthen our Journalism Awards Program, Vocational Awareness and Enrichment projects, Employer- Employee Relations activities, and Teacher Values-Formation and Development Program.

• We shall send graduate Filipino scholars to universities abroad, group study exchange teams to foreign lands and delegates to international conventions and seminars.

• We shall sponsor a new program of promoting and safeguarding overseas employment.

• We shall sponsor the RCM National Oratorical Contest, RCM National Essay Writing Contest and the RCM Metro Manila Inter-Collegiate Choral Festival.

• We shall continue supporting the Boy Scouts movement, the Philippine Cancer Society, the Philippine Band of Mercy, the various Rotaract and InterAct clubs we established in several schools, as well as our Career Guidance Program for high school seniors.

•We shall establish and sponsor for the first time this year, the Search for the Most Outstanding Public Servants.

## Manila Rotary is Mostly and Most Importantly for the Poor, the Afflicted and the Weak.

THIRD. Manila Rotary is mostly and most importantly for the poor, the afflicted and the weak. That is why:

•We shall, under the leadership of our more that 40 physician-members, sponsor six medico-surgical missions to the poor areas of our country and provide free medical and surgical services and medicines to about 4,800 poor patients.

•We shall provide free eye operation at our RCM Eye Operating Center and sponsor, an outreach eye clinic to reach 10,000 patients in 12 towns and cities as well as establish an eye bank to offer literally, the gift of sight to the blind.

• We shall, in cooperation with the World Health Organization and the Manila Health Department, launch an immunization campaign against polio and hepatitis for at least 1,000 children in eight health centers in Manila.

• We shall sponsor free health and nutrition education and child care for 300 families in Sampaloc, Manila, under our Paluwagang Pangkalusugan sa Barangay Program.

• We shall provide free heart surgery to the poor, including the implanting of heart pacemakers under the "Save A Heart, Save A Life" project.

• We shall intensify our drug abuse prevention program especially among the young. This project was especially endorsed by President Corazon C. Aquino.

• We shall enable 1,000 patients to hear better through our Hearing Conservation Project.

• We commit to provide even more material, medical, social and spiritual assistance to the poor barangays in San Andres Bukid, Manila, which we adopted through our Rotary Village Corps.

• We shall provide scholarships for 300 poor students in Don Bosco Technical Institute in Tondo, Manila, as well as 50 college scholarship grants in selected schools in Metro Manila. In addition, the Cityland Development Corporation headed by Rotarians Stephen Roxas and Andy Liuson has agreed to give an initial P 200,000 to fund additional scholarships in computer education.

• We shall provide home, food, medical care and education to Manila's street children, through our "Sagip Kabataan" Center on UN Avenue and in Tagaytay City.

• We shall sponsor a "Mini-Olympics" and visits to various newspaper offices for poor newsboys to show them that we care and love them.

• We shall set up two new major shoeshine stands in Makati and Manila and thereby provide a means of livelihood to several poor boys.

• We shall put up a pilot livelihood and self-help project for the poor in Sampaloc, which could be repeated in several other parts of the country by other Rotary clubs.

• We shall actively promote a clean and healthy environment through various media, consistent with Rotary International's "Preserve Planet Earth" program.

• We shall activate our Emergency Relief Group to bring cheer and goodies to poor families this Christmas and to provide material assistance in case of typhoons and floods this rainy season.

• We commit, through our re-invigorated Social Justice Committee, to give more to those who have less in life, by motivating and encouraging our more privileged members to give more generously to their employees, neighbors and household helps.

## 1990 - 1991 Budget

The senior members of our club will probably note from the foregoing that, under my incumbency, RCM will continue, and in fact even expand, all our existing projects. In addition, we shall be embarking on several new projects.

To fund all these projects and activities, we need at least P10 million. The biggest budget RCM had in the past was P5 million.

In spite of this, your board of directors decided not to increase membership dues, which will total only P3 million. In other words, to meet our budget, we need to raise at least P7 million from sources other than membership dues. May I cite Sonny Belmonte and Roger Cabunag for agreeing to spearhead the fund-raising for these projects.

## Raison d'Etre of Manila Rotary

Ladies and Gentlemen, Manila Rotary is strong and sturdy because it has a three-point VISION, because it stands on a triad, because it is structured like a pyramid. But as you will undoubtedly note, our most important commitment is that Manila Rotary is for the poor, the afflicted and the weak. This is the base, the most important component, the driving force of our VISION, our triad and our pyramid. As your president, I vow to channel our major treasures, time and talent in helping our less fortunate brothers. To me, this is the raison d'etre, the reason for being of Manila Rotary - to help those who cannot help themselves and to help those who cannot help us back in return.

If the rich who are few will not help the poor who are many, there will come a time when the few who are filled cannot escape the wrath of the many who are hungry.

Feeding the hungry, clothing the naked, comforting the afflicted, sheltering the homeless and protecting the oppressed is not an option but a duty and responsibility of the well-to-do, the talented and the privileged. Indeed, when we, in our utter nakedness, shall face our Creator and Ultimate Judge on that inevitable day, He will not ask us how many digits are in our bank accounts; or how many diplomas have been conferred on us; or how many plaques were awarded to us; or even, how many times we have gone to church. Rather, our Creator and Master will ask us whether we fed the hungry, clothed the naked, sheltered the homeless, comforted the afflicted, healed the sick and protected the oppressed. Jesus Christ added that whenever we do these acts of charity and love for the needy, we really do them for our God. "I assure you," He said, "as often as you did it for the least of My brothers, you did it for Me.”

## Rotary Maxims have Definite Theological Roots

"Service Above Self. He Profits Most Who Serves Best." In invoking these familiar Rotary maxim, Paul Harris, the founder of Rotary International, was mouthing not just a capitalist doctrine but a spiritual truth.

This is the reason why the incoming team has adopted LOVE GOD, SERVE MAN as our new credo. It is really a theological restatement of Paul Harris' maxims. This credo summarizes our whole being and purpose on earth - to glorify God by serving our fellowmen.

After His resurrection from the dead, Jesus Christ showed Himself several times to His disciples. On His third appearance, Jesus asked this question of Simon Peter, "Simon, son of John, do you love Me?" to which the latter replied, "Yes Lord, You know that I love You." At which Jesus commanded, "Feed My lambs."

A second time, He put the same question "Simon, son of John, do you love Me?" "Yes, Lord" Peter said, "You know that I love You." Jesus replied, "Feed My sheep."

The third time Jesus asked him, "Simon, son of John, do you love Me?" Peter was hurt because Jesus had asked him a third time. So he said to Him: "Lord You know everything. You know well that I love You." Again, Jesus said to him, "Feed My sheep."

To the point of being repetitious, Jesus asked Peter, the head of His church, whether he loved Him. And three times, Jesus emphasized that love for God required tending to the needs of His flock, particularly the young and the helpless.

The unmistakable divine message is that love of God can best be expressed by our service to man, particularly to the poor, the afflicted and the weak, who can neither help themselves or help us back in return.

In this message of our Lord, we clearly see that our Rotary service for the benefit of those who have less in this temporal life can become our key to the gate of our own eternal life. In this light, Rotary work ceases to be merely for the here and now but can be our anointment for the hereafter and forever.

The new president of Rotary International, Paolo V. C. da Costa of Brazil, has chosen "HONOR ROTARY WITH FAITH AND ENTHUSIASM" as this year's world-wide theme of Rotary. With this theme, President Paolo is asking every Rotarian to help in the great effort to "Preserve Planet Earth." We in the Rotary Club of Manila hereby solemnly respond to this call with an even deeper commitment. We pledge to HONOR ROTARY by loving God with child-like FAITH and serving man with contagious ENTHUSIASM.

## Manila Rotary is Committed to the Cause of Peace

Ladies and gentlemen, the Rotary Club of Manila is proud to be a part of the world-wide Rotary movement to which it owes filiation and loyalty. Every Manila Rotarian is equally and singularly proud of the fact that the first and only Filipino president of Rotary International is a member of our Club. I refer, of course, to the low-profile PCGG Chairman, Mat Caparas.

The Rotary Club of Manila is also a part of the Filipino nation and, therefore, it pledges its loyalty and allegiance to our country and to our Constitution. And more than that, the Rotary Club of Manila is deeply committed to the cause of PEACE, which is ardently promoted by both our country and our mother organization. Peace is not just the absence of violence or the eerie stalemate produced by a balance of nuclear terror, or the silent subservience that is imposed by dictators. Rather, the PEACE that we strive for is the fruit of social justice and genuine love for our fellowmen. It is giving every man his due. It is helping him to live with dignity. It is enabling him to enjoy the minimum necessities of human life.

It is the product of what the late Justice Jose P. Laurel defines it as "the humanization of all laws and the equalization of social and economic forces. It is what the Catholic Church refers to as the solidarity of those who have more with those who have less for the latter's total development. In the words of Pope John Paul I in his very recent encyclical Solicitudo Rei Socialis, “true (peace) must be based on the love of God and neighbor."

All these pronouncements tell us that to achieve peace - that kind of peace that comes out of social justice, total development and solidarity, we are tasked to serve our neighbor out of love for God. In this sense, the Rotary Club of Manila truly contributes to the cause of PEACE by relentlessly pursuing its credo - LOVE GOD, SERVE MAN.

## Conclusion: 'Love God, Serve Man' is the Cornerstone of Rotary Service

Fellow Rotarians, ladies and gentlemen, in closing let me reiterate my VISION for our Club: Manila Rotary stands for our members, our colleagues and our guests; Manila Rotary aims for excellence in the professions, in business and in education. But mostly, mainly and fervently, Manila Rotary is for the poor, the afflicted and the weak. This is the cornerstone of my presidency. This is our way of helping our country, our church and our mother organization in their quest for true peace. Upon this credo, LOVE GOD, SERVE MAN, I shall be judged, not only in the Rotary Club of Manila but I dare say, in my whole unworthy life here on earth.

Fellow Rotarians and friends, may I invite all of you to please rise and to begin our quest for PEACE by reciting together the Prayer of St. Francis of Assisi:

"Lord, make me an instrument of your peace;

where there is hatred, let me bring love;

where there is injury, pardon;

where there is doubt, faith;

where there is despair, hope;

where there is darkness, light;

and where there is sadness, joy.

O Divine Master, grant that I may not so much seek

to be consoled as to console;

to be understood as to understand;

to be loved as to love;

for it is in giving that we receive;

it is in pardoning that we are pardoned, and

it is in dying that we are born to eternal life."

I thank you.

## Summary

Chapter 1 of 'Love God, Serve Man' is the address CJP delivered on July 12, 1990 at the Manila Hotel Fiesta Pavilion on his induction as president of the Rotary Club of Manila, where he announced the credo that gives the book its title. He begins with humility and history: he had never been a candidate, since under an election procedure unique in all of Rotary the top vote-getter for the board is automatically proclaimed president; his predecessors included Senate President Gil J. Puyat, Justice George A. Malcolm and Carlos P. Romulo; and the Club, at 455 members, is the oldest in Asia and the largest outside the United States, which he says has loaded a heavy burden upon his lean and unworthy shoulders. Elected as early as December 1989, he spent seven months planning with his team and produced a printed Program of Action built on a three-point VISION, structured like a triad and a pyramid: Manila Rotary is for its members, colleagues and guests; for excellence in the professions, business, education and government; and mostly and most importantly for the poor, the afflicted and the weak. He enumerates the programs under each point -- weekly meetings and membership targets, the 4-way test and journalism and scholarship programs, and a long list of charitable projects from medico-surgical missions and a new eye bank to immunization drives, Save A Heart, Save A Life, Sagip Kabataan, newsboys' Mini-Olympics and shoeshine stands -- and states the budget frankly: P10 million needed against a previous high of P5 million, with dues unchanged and P7 million to be raised. The raison d'etre, he says, is to help those who cannot help themselves and to help those who cannot help us back in return, for if the rich who are few will not help the poor who are many, the few who are filled cannot escape the wrath of the many who are hungry. He then grounds Rotary's maxims theologically in Christ's threefold command to Peter to feed My sheep, defines peace as the fruit of social justice, and closes by submitting to be judged upon the credo and inviting all to recite the Prayer of St. Francis of Assisi.
