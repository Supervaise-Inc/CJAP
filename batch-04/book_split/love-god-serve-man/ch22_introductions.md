# Chapter 22: Introductions

(Editor's note: Attorney Panganiban is as much a sought-after introducer of guest speakers as he is himself a powerful main speaker. Here are three samples of introductions he has delivered: [1] for former Senate President Jovito R. Salonga; [2] for journalist Maximo V. Soliven, and [3] for Dr. Jesus P. Estanislao.)

1. Introduction for Senate President Jovito R. Salonga, delivered on March 15, 1989 at the Century Park Sheraton Hotel Ballroom. In our long, heroic struggle against oppression, tyranny and injustice, the Filipino people were fortunate that during times of crises, men of integrity, wisdom and courage rise up undaunted and unafraid to provide hope and direction to our country and our countrymen. Thus, during the Philippine Revolution at the end of the last century, heroes like Jose Rizal and Apolinario Mabini came to the fore and supplied the brains, ideology and spirit behind our nationalistic endeavors. After our country was ravaged by World War II, stout-hearted men like Jose P. Laurel and Claro M. Recto provided the wisdom and direction that accompanied our attempts at national identification and reconstruction. And now, after an epic battle against a corrupt and decadent dictatorship, our guest speaker, a man of vision and a man of God, has emerged in our milieu leading his peers not only in intellectual statesmanship but also in political charisma unmatched in our country's electoral history. Our guest's curriculum vitae has been reproduced by SKAL President Purito Saba and has been distributed to all of you. I will not bore you by repeating what you can read. However, I will

try to introduce him as I personally know him, first as my professor and dean when I was a law student 30 years ago; second, as my first boss when I joined his law firm after graduation; third, as someone who took over his teaching chores at Far Eastern University, which he had to give up to attend to his many duties as congressman of the second district of Rizal, and finally, as one who has assiduously admired and cherished his life and career.

In spite of my many years of exposure to him, I am still mesmerized and awed by his very presence. He speaks with a forceful tremolo that spellbinds his hearers with his wisdom, sincerity, honesty and credibility.

Our guest speaker has the consistent habit of being number one in everything he did. He topped the bar examination in 1943, was awarded the Ambrose Gherini Prize by the Yale Law School for writing the best paper in the field of international law upon finishing his doctorate, and made political history by topping the national senatorial elections three times - first in 1965, then 1971 and now in 1987. No one has ever achieved this feat.

As a public official, integrity, performance and hard work are his credo. While he was head of one of the most sensitive offices of the Cory government, the Presidential Commission on Good Government, the vaunted money, men and machines of Marcos and his cronies were used in a massive effort to defile him, yet his reputation remained unblemished and ever immaculate.

As a man, his life is exemplary. He is a dedicated husband, a caring father, a faithful friend and a devoted man of God. His ways are simple. He lives an almost ascetic life. He does not smoke, does not drink, does not gamble. He does not even own the house where he stays in Pasig, his hometown. Indeed, money, worldly pleasure, titles and honors have no relevance for him. He never compromises his principles and values for convenience, friendship, wealth or power.

In 1971, during the miting de avance of the Liberal Party in the now famous Plaza Miranda, a grenade was thrown at the stage. It exploded and killed eight persons. In the case of our guest speaker, a thousand pieces of shrapnel pierced his body and at one point, his blood pressure was zero. In spite of this devilish attempt to snuff out his mortal life, he recovered and by a miracle of God he remained in complete command of his most precious gift - his towering intellect. Upon waking up in his hospital bed, he reflected: "There must be a reason why God spared my life."

Ladies and gentlemen, we now know the reason why the Lord spared his life - he was destined to be the brilliant mind that would provide the vision and the direction in our people's renewed quest for justice and nationhood.

Fellow Skalleagues, it is my honor and privilege to present to you a model public servant, a man of destiny, a man of God, the third highest official of our land, Senate President JOVITO R.

SALONGA.

2. Introduction for Maximo V. Soliven, delivered on September 30, 1992 at the Hotel Intercontinental Manila Ballroom MAXIMO V. SOLIVEN, or Max as he is fondly called is a legend of our time. He has had a many-splendored career. To call him the Most Outstanding Journalist of our time as we did in the Rotary Club of Manila is to proclaim only a partial truth about him. But a truth nonetheless. Because six days a week, he writes the longest and perhaps most persuasive English-language column in our country. In addition, he also writes a daily column in Tagalog in the Ang Pilipino Ngayon. At one time or another, during his checkered journalist's past, he wrote for Newsweek, the New York Times, the South China Morning Post of Hong Kong, the Bangkok Post and several other national and international publications.

To call him the most famous CEO in the publishing business is to say another partial truth. But a truth nonetheless. For aside from writing his two daily columns, he is concurrently publisher and chairman of the editorial board of the Philippine Star. Aside from that, he is also board chairman, CEO, publisher and editor in chief of Eastgate International, which publishes, among other publications, the multi-awarded Mabuhay, in-flight magazine of Philippine Airlines. In 1985 Max became the founding publisher of the Philippine Daily Inquirer which was established to fight the Marcos dictatorship and which, at that time, achieved a daily circulation of 350,000, the biggest in the Philippine newspaper industry. Years before that, at age 27, Max achieved the distinction of being the youngest publisher and editor-in-chief of the premartial law Evening News.

Max has been the recipient of the highest awards given by almost every institution which grants recognition to media practitioners including the National Press Club, Catholic Mass Media Awards and the prestigious Rotary Club of Manila. His many foreign awards were capped by a decoration given by President Francois Mitterand of France, namely, that of Chevalier (knight) of the National Order of Merit of France.

He obtained his bachelor's degree from Ateneo, his master's in communication and political philosophy from Fordham and undertook further graduate studies at Johns Hopkins University and at Harvard University under Henry Kissinger.

He has interviewed and rubbed elbows with the best and brightest leaders of this century, like China's Deng Xiao Ping, India's late Prime Ministers Jawaharlal Nehru and Rajiv Gandhi, USA's John F. Kennedy, Indonesia's President Sukarno, Israel's Golda Meir, Japan's ex-Prime Ministers Kaifu and Takeshita. So, too, he has the singular distinction of being kicked out of South Vietnam by the dictator Ngo Dinh Diem, out of Burma by militarist General Ne Win, out of Singapore by strongman Lee Kuan Yew. But the "trouble" he is really famous for in this colorful career and

which makes him an authentic hero of our time is his arrest and detention in maximum security prison as the cellmate of the late Ninoy Aquino. After he was released from jail, he was forbidden to leave Metro Manila for three years, banned from foreign travel for seven years and prohibited from writing or giving interviews for a parallel seven years until martial law was officially lifted. I believe that this last prohibition truly pained him. Detention, hunger, tear gas, torture and prison, Max will endure, but stopping him from writing and especially from talking - that's just too much. For Max is one of the very few who cannot be stopped from writing and speaking. Truly, he can speak and write with lucidity, with depth, with humor, with meaning, with eloquence and always with gusto.

All the political parties, major and minor, now and in the past - with the possible exception of Marcos' KBL, have enticed him to run for public office. But he says he has not lost his sanity yet and has consistently refused any government job.

Ladies and gentlemen, words are truly inadequate to state the whole truth about our guest. But let me do something better - by presenting him in flesh and blood, the many-splendored columnist, editor, author, publisher, CEO, wit, public speaker and the most sparkling star of the Star, Maximo V. Soliven.

3. Introduction for Dr. Jesus P. Estanislao delivered on December 14, 1993 before the 19th Philippine Business Conference sponsored by the Philippine Chamber of Commerce and Industry at the Philippine International Convention Center I am truly honored to have been given this opportunity to introduce our distinguished guest, who to me is the epitome of a God-loving person and model public servant. Many have disputed the possibility of pursuing the holiness of God while achieving excellence in one's chosen career, but our guest is a quiet, living,

day-to-day witness that indeed, one can be a saint in the midst of the noise and banging of the world. He has forsaken the allure and enjoyment of worldly possessions and marital security. Instead, he has chosen to walk the straight and narrow path with determination and perseverance. For in Christ Jesus, our guest truly lives and moves and has his being.

Educated in the University of San Carlos, Fordham University and Harvard University, where he earned his doctoral degree in Economics, our guest speaker is currently president of the AFTA Advisory Commission as well as of the Southeast Asian Science Foundation, Inc. Prior to this, he served as secretary of finance from 1990 to 1992, and simultaneously chaired the Land Bank of the Philippines.

His previous involvements in the private sector included, among others, the chairmanship of the Philippine Cement Corporation, Associated Bank, Cebu Plaza Hotel and First Pacific Capital Corporation. He was a member of the board of the Manila Electric Company, the Philippine Commercial International Bank, First Philippine Holdings Inc., Century Bank, Asean Finance Corporation and Bacnotan Consolidated Industries, Inc.

A dedicated academician, he was professorial lecturer at the University of the Philippines, professor of economic policy at the Center for Research and Communication and a teaching fellow at Harvard.

In his work in the government and in the private sector, he has not been involved in any matter that placed his integrity or judgment in doubt. He is truly held in high esteem by his constituencies as well as the general public.

Ladies and gentlemen, may I present a dedicated educator, a knowledgeable business executive, an honest public official and most of all, a humble servant of God, Dr. Jesus P. Estanislao.
