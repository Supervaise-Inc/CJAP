# Reforming the Judiciary -- Ch. 9: A Centennial Toast to Justice and Justices
Date:       2002-12-01
Publisher:  Supreme Court Printing Press
Source:     Reforming the Judiciary, Supreme Court Printing Press — ch. 9
By: Artemio V. Panganiban

During the last two years, the Supreme Court celebrated its centennial by sponsoring more than fifty activities and projects. Launched on June 9, 2000, the celebrations were planned to culminate on the exact 100th birthday of the Court on June 11, 2001. However, they were extended another year. And so today, June 11, 2002, the Supreme Court’s jubilee comes to a formal close.

The celebratory activities had two goals: first, to communicate to the whole country the role of the judiciary in nation-building during the last 100 years; and second, to focus on and encourage participation in the ongoing judicial reform program of the Supreme Court. Our overall theme has been “Katarungan at Bayan Magpakailanman.” By this, we emphasized our allegiance to one cause, justice; and to one entity, the nation.

That I have been asked to chair the centennial celebrations has brought me distinct honor and pleasure, not only because of the opportunity to spearhead the unique programs we have sponsored, but also because of the joy I experienced while working with the dedicated, efficient and cooperative members of the Executive Committee.

Truly, the celebrations were successful and exultant, not because of the Execom chairman, but because of those who steadfastly and persistently labored to implement and carry out every centenary project we envisioned.

I do not have the time to mention all the more than 05 centenary activities we sponsored. Neither will I be able to name all the hundreds of men and women - both from the court system and outside it - who worked diligently, freely and wholeheartedly to make our celebration the exultant success that it has been. However, let me in the limited time available summarize here some of our activities.

First. Let me cite our highly acclaimed Centenary Lecture Series, which has whipped up unprecedented attention and appreciation from our intellectual public. Ably led by the indomitable Justice Ameurfina A. Melencio Herrera and coordinated by the equally affable Dr. Purificacion V. Quisumbing, the Lecture Series - 14 individual lectures in all - covered diverse topics in lectures delivered by distinguished and scholarly speakers, both local and foreign: from constitutionalism to biotechnology, from paperless courts and electronic evidence to Shari’a and Muslim law, from local phenomena like People Power to foreign judicial dispensation in countries like Hungary and Israel.

Second. We held a National Speech Festival. Through debates, essays and oratorical contests, we emphasized the importance of language mastery and effective communication, both of the written and the spoken varieties. This festival is anchored on the postulate that mastery of forensic and writing skills enables men and women of the law to discover the truth, prove their legal theories, formulate legal principles and render justice to all.

These forensic activities were organized and implemented for the Executive Committee by the Philippine Association of Law Schools chaired by then Dean Hernando B. Perez. Upon his appointment as justice secretary, he was succeeded by Dean Romulo V. Borja. Ably assisting were all the law deans in the country, especially Deans Ponciano Subido, Norberto Gonzales, Raul Pangalangan, and Andres Bautista.

Third. We launched the First National Moot Court Competition under the inspired leadership of former Dean and now Foreign Affairs Undersecretary Merlin M. Magallona. Nationwide in scope, this contest aimed to promote virtual-reality legal advocacy amongst law students. A simulation of oral arguments before our Supreme Court, it sought to enhance the specific skills needed by a great lawyer - skills like legal research, pleading preparation, knowledge of law, mastery of specific facts, teamwork and advocacy. As contradistinguished from debates, the moot court included unrehearsed interactions between the judges and the "mooters." That the First National Moot Court Competition" was an unqualified success is good news; but the better news is that the Philippine Association of Law Schools, the Integrated Bar of the Philippines, and the Association of Law Students of the Philippines have agreed to continue holding it on a biennial basis. What is more, they have agreed to eventually include the moot court as part of the law school curriculum.

Fourth. We undertook several public information programs and produced various public relations tools to promote our judicial reform projects. These PR tools included the publication of the Centenary Reader, a threevolume anthology featuring excerpts from the best Supreme Court decisions over the last 100 years - decisions that have become veritable sources of history, philosophy and literature. I would like to acknowledge publicly the help of the "Legal Write" group headed by Mr. Rogelio Pantaleon, which produced the book at no expense to the Court; as well as the book editors led by Justice Abraham F. Sarmiento, Justice Camilo D. Quiason, Mr. Alfred Luson, Prof. Gemino H. Abad and Ms Asuncion Albert. I also cite the legal research team headed by our own Atty. Edna E. Diño.

Furthermore, we have caused the publication of several other books, including those about the lives and careers of Chief Justices Querube Makalintal and Ramon Avanceña,13 Justice Cecilia Muñoz-Palma's book The Mirror of My Soul, and one written by yours truly entitled ACentenary of Justice. We are also producing centennial editions of the Philippine Reports and several commemorative books, including one marking the Centenary Lecture Series.

Similarly, we have produced two versions of a TV documentary entitled "Bulwark of Justice." It was conceptualized by a committee headed by Justice Artu B . Buena. In connection with this, I am proud to announce officially that another video presentation prepared by the Execom, entitled "A Celebration of Justice," was given the prestigious Gawad Oscar M . Florendo Award! by the Public Relations Org of the Philippines (PROP).

Fifth. The celebrations were not confined to Metro Manila. Appropriate centennial celebrations were held in all judicial regions all over the country under the watch of then Court Administrator Alfredo L. Benipayo, then acting Court Administrator Zenaida N. Elepaño and current Court Administrator Presbitero J. Velasco Jr.; and the active cooperation of all the RTC and the MC/MeTC executive judges, as well as of the Philippine Judges Association headed by then Judge and now Court of Appeals Justice Danilo B. Pine. Apart from. parades, sports contests and cultural programs, these regional activities zeroed in on our reform program as it applied to the trial courts.

Sixth. The retired magistrates of the Court were not forgotten. Centennial awards were given to them during a well-attended luncheon at the Manila Hotel. Organized by the Association of Retired Supreme Court Justices headed by Justice Abraham F . Sarmiento, a Book and Photo Exhibit featuring the lives and works of former members of the Court was also held.

Seventh. Religious and spiritual activities, like ecumenical services and Masses, were included throughout the two-year celebrations. After close consultations with all major religious denominations, the "Centennial Prayer for the Courts" was composed by the Execom. This prayer, which was recited at the beginning of this program, is now used not only in the Supreme Court but in all courts all over the country.

Eighth. A Judiciary Sportsfest featuring tennis and golf was also organized by our Execom Vice Chairman, Justice Leonardo A. Quisumbing, with the full support of the Judiciary Sports Club. Not to be outdone, our employees in the entire judiciary also held a Centennial Sportsfest in various categories like basketball and volleyball.

Ninth. The SC employees were aptly remembered through a full afternoon cultural program. Organized by a committee headed by Justice Minerva P. GonzagaReyes, the program was held in the Court of Appeals auditorium. A special luncheon at the Holiday Inn was also organized by Attys. Luzviminda D. Puno and Marissa D. Villarama. Awards were given to outstanding former employees during this occasion.

Tenth. Likewise, special once-in-a-hundred-years Centennial Awards for Judicial Excellence were handed out. The awards were given during appropriate ceremonies at the Westin Philippine Plaza Hotel after an extensive nationwide search spearheaded by Senior Justice Josue N. Bellosillo and Justice Jose Y. Feria (ret.). As a measure of the esteem and value given to these awards, Judge Mario L. Guarina III, the winner of the highest award for regional trial court judges, was recently promoted to the appellate court.

Eleventh. With the active assistance of the Court of Appeals —especially through former Presiding Justice . Montoya, former PJ Ma. Alicia Austria-Martinez and acting PJ Cancio C. Garcia - we have commissioned the design and the construction of the statues of Chief Justices Cayetano Arellano and Jose Abad Santos. Both of these statues will be installed on the front courtyard of the Supreme Court. Similarly, we are commissioning mural paintings depicting the history of the Court, to be installed in the lobby of the Court's Main Building. These statues and murals, as well as the three-volume Centenary Reader which I mentioned earlier, are actively supervised by the Philippine Judiciary Foundation headed by Madam Justice Carolina Griño-Aquino (ret.).

Twelfth. With funding from the Australian government and the Asia Foundation, the Benchbook for Judges and the Benchbook on Penalties have been produced. Now commonly referred to as the bibles of judges, these books contain all relevant materials that a judge may need in order to rule instantly on fine points of law, which may be raised during a trial. At present, training courses on the use of these volumes are being held under Philja's sponsorship.

Thirteenth. After the successful launching of a pilot program held in Manila, Cebu and Davao from March 26 to April 6 last year, we are now following up the full implementation of mediation as a tool for decongesting court dockets. It is interesting to note that during the pilot program, which we called Settlement Week, 1,469 - or 81 percent of 1,816 court cases referred for mediation - were successfully settled. To institutionalize this program, the Philippine Mediation Center was established on April 6, 2001.

Apart from these foregoing major centenary• activities which have been or are about to be completed, we also envisioned, started, implemented or monitored several judicial reform projects which are still ongoing and will be continued even after the centenary celebrations  are ended today. Notable among these are the following.

One. Realizing that delay in case adjudication is a major area of concern, the Supreme Court has implemented the "Zero Backlog" project. It is a continuing program to decide all cases within the constitutional limit of twenty-four months after a case 1s deemed submitted for resolution. Following the High Tribunal's example, the Court of Appeals — at the initiative of then Presiding Justice Ma. Alicia AustriaMartinez - launched its own Zero Backlog program. Similarly, Court Administrator Presbitero J. Velasco Jr. and his deputies are setting up a program to reduce case backlogs in the trial courts.

Two. To keep up with legal, economic, scientific, commercial and jurisprudential developments, the Supreme Court Committee on Rules chaired by Justice Reynato S. Puno has updated the Rules on Criminal Procedure. It has also produced entirely new sets of procedural guidelines to regulate novel matters like electronic evidence, corporate rehabilitation, juveniles in conflict with the law, family disputes, intellectual property and so on.

Three. The Mandatory Continuing Legal Education (MCLE) Board has been organized, with Justice Carolina Griño-Aquino as chairperson. The MCLE Board has started implementing its program, even as various duly appointed legal education providers have initiated special seminars and courses required by law. MCLE is a reform project that aims to elevate professional and ethical standards of lawyers.

Four. As a concrete measure to implement the computerization of the courts, 1,500 personal computers have been purchased and are being distributed to all regional trial courts and selected metropolitan and municipal trial courts nationwide. These computers are equipped with the software Lex Libris to facilitate the research work of trial courts. The computerization activities were monitored by our Execom subcommittee led by Justice Consuelo Y. Santiago.

Five. We have inspired the organization of the Foundation for the Enhancement of Legal Education chaired by Atty. Ricardo J. Romulo. The foundation is now in the process of raising funds to enable it to establish national professorial chairs in various law schools and to grant scholarships to deserving but poor students.

Six, The Supreme Court premises and buildings are now undergoing renovations and repairs. I refer specifically to the renovation of the Session Hall where we are holding this closing ceremony, the en banc and the Division conference rooms, the main lobby, the foyer and the front courtyard. We are also in the process of constructing a Division Hearing Room, expanding the library, restoring the old Session Hall and the old Office of the Chief Justice, and relocating the Memorabilia Room. This huge responsibility is directly under Justice Vicente V. Mendoza.

Seven. The construction of the interiors and the provision of high technology facilities for the new Cent enni al Building are complete. In fact, the inauguration was held this morning as part of our centenary closing celebrations. The Committee on Halls of Justice headed by Justice Santiago M. Kapunan is also supervising the construction of a model Hall of Justice in Manila to be located at the old Jai Alai Fronton on Taft Avenue, as well as the construction of a multipurpose parking building near the Court of Appeals.

Eight. In the works also is the construction of two additional cottages in our Baguio compound to replace the ones destroyed by the earthquake ten years ago. Two other guest cottages are also in the works, while the staff house construction is almost finished. These projects are keenly monitored by Attys. Luzviminda D. Puno and Marissa D. Villarama.

Nine. Upon the retirement of Justice Bernardo P. Pardo, Justice Antonio T. Carpio took over active leadership in implementing a mass housing program for our employees. Being finalized with the assistance of the Government Service Insurance System (GSIS) and the Pag-ibig, the judiciary housing project will likely be located in Cavite along the planned extended line of the Light Railway Transit (LRT).

Ten. A major project that is in need of funding is the construction of the Philja Center in Tagaytay. Requiring at least P500 million to realize, this Center has captured the imagination of all who are interested in continuing judicial education. The project is awesome, but I am confident that, under the leadership of Chief Justice Hilario G. Davide Jr and Chancellor Ameurfina A. Melencio Herrera, the Center will soon rise on Philja's three-hectare site in Tagaytay.

Ladies and gentlemen, these are only some of the ongoing and completed major programs and projects that have been started or implemented during our two-year centennial celebrations. There are many more, but limitations of time and attention span prevent me from discussing all of them here.

Suffice it to say that a more extended report of our centennial activities is contained in the Centenary Yearbook prepared by our Public Information Office headed by Atty. Ismael G. Khan Jr., who is also our Execom executive officer.

In the meantime, may I reiterate my sincere thanks to Chief Justice Hilario Gi. Davide Jr. and my colleagues in the Court for giving me the rare opportunity to serve in the celebration of our once-in-a-lifetime centenary. Although all our centennial projects and activities have been acclaimed as successful, I concede that some of them may not be perfect. I personally accept responsibility for all these imperfections. As I always say, the buck stops at the desk of the Execom chairman. However, in all sincerity, I give credit to the members of the Supreme Court and the Executive Committee for the success of our endeavors. Finally, may I thank our Lord Jesus Christ who has constantly inspired and assisted all of us.

As I close this Report, let me invite all of you to toast this singular celebration of justice in our country. I say Mabuhay to the Supreme Court — may it continue to be the bulwark of justice, the conscience of society, the guardian of the Constitution and the seat of freedom in our land. I also say Mabhay to all the justices of the Supreme Court, incumbent and retired - the gallant and wise men and women who have graced its august

halls from 1901 to the present. May they always remain pillars of integrity, independence, intelligence and industry in the dispensation of equal and speedy justice for all.

Maraming salamat po.
