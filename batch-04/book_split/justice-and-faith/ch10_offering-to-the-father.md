# Chapter 10: Offering to the Father

“Father, into your hands, I commend my spirit.” With these last words, Jesus died on the cross. Since then and for two thousand years - up to today and till the end of time - these eternal words will reverberate in the whole universe. Countless homilies and reflections have been written and delivered on these famous last words. The common message is that Christ’s perfect obedience to the Father in heaven led him to the cross and to His death, a death He, as the perfect human servant of the Father, freely and happily chose. As His loyal disciples and followers, we are asked to do no less, we are asked to imitate Jesus, to put ourselves in His place, to commend our spirit, and in every manner of speaking, to die for the eternal Father.

Two weeks ago, my six-year-old grandson Miggy proudly showed me a certificate and an academic award as an outstanding pupil given him by Southridge School, showing that he successfully passed preschool and was then qualified to enroll in Grade I. I congratulated him and asked him how he did it. He beamed and replied, “Lolo, I studied hard, did not watch television except during holidays, woke up early during school days, and did my homework every day." Then he got back his certificate and award and placed them at our little altar at home. My grandson, in his own innocent way, deprived himself of his little pleasures like watching his favorite TV shows and waking up late, studied hard and then offered his reward to the Lord. Yes, he commended his work to the Father. And by enrolling in Grade I and by promising to study even harder and thus depriving himself of more playful pleasures, he - in his own small way - intensified his personal sacrifice to be able to commend to God a greater part of himself and his work.

True indeed, in our own different ways, we imitate Christ. We experience our own calvaries and our own deaths. Some set aside the allures of the world — power, possession and prestige - to find their calvaries in permanent isolation to be able to contemplate and worship God all the days and times of their mortal lives. Some leave father, mother, brother, sister, girlfriend or boyfriend to become, as holy men and women of the cloth, totally available to Him. Missi onarie s coura geous ly sail the seven seas to preach and propagate His gospel to the world. Still some others — as devoted flagellants - take His word literally and beat their backs with glassimbedded whips or even get themselves physically nailed to the cross during Holy Week.

However, not all of us are called to be hermits or priests or numeraries and nuns, or missionaries, or even flagellants. But all of us, each one of us, very much like my grandson, are called to die to our sinfulness and offer to the Lord our daily work, our career, our leisure, our everyday activities. Each of us must bring Christ to our workplaces, to the board rooms, to schools, to the streets and every place where we are. As Mother Teresa aptly said, "We are all called to be Christ to others so that others may become Christ to still many others."

God has made us different from one another and has placed us in our unique environment: sometimes apart, sometimes alike, but never completely and identically the same. Our Heavenly Father expects us to be like Christ while living in our unique situation, to offer the best of what we are and what we can do, for Him who is our Source and our Creator. Our Heavenly Father wants us to imitate Christ - to commend our spirits , our bodies, our possessions, our careers, our time, our jobs, our very selves to Him. That is why He created us - to glorify Him with all that we are and with all that we ever hope to be.

Now, when we give something to our loved ones, we want to give only the best. When it is the birthday of someone we love or esteem or respect, we carefully choose a gift, something the recipient would truly want and appreciate. We wrap it carefully and deliver it with the best prose from our heart. Who is the mother who will deny her child the best of this life even when, in doing so, she deprives herself of that which she gives away?

So it is with our God. We give our hearts to him in love and in sacrifice. We offer Him to him that which He said He truly wants from us: a virtuous life lived according to His beatitudes.

When a student studies hard and earns the best grades possible, he offers these to the Lord; when a policeman, with honesty and dedication, patrols his beat and fosters peace and security in his neighborhood, he brings his work to the Lord; when a housewife struggles with her household budget and prepares meals for her family lovingly and tenderly, she serves the Lord; when a husband works and lives honestly, faithfully and conscientiously, he commends his life to Jesus; when a businessman sells quality goods at reasonable prices, not taking advantage of the poor but helping them meet their needs, he pleases the Father; when a government functionary discharges his duties in all integrity and industry, he lifts up his work to Him as Christ did on the cross. Our God wants only virtue and goodness, never sinfulness, sloth or neglect. So, we offer only that which glorifies Him according to His command, never that which debases and dishonors Him according to man's deceitful mind.

Brothers and sisters, St. Augustine — the sinner-saint - once said that our hearts are restless until they rest in God. Our desires and wants can never be satisfied by this imperfect world. Only the perfect God can truly fill our minds and spirits. We are all humans who, at one time or another, have been injured, maimed, insulted, assaulted, afflicted persecuted, deprived, broken, abandoned and crushed. All of us have suffered illness, pain, spiritual emptiness, psychological affliction and social deprivation. We are all members of a ragtag army of walking wounded, pretending to be whole and healthy, but in truth longing for the healing, the love and the peace that will come only when we offer all these pains and sufferings to our God, only when our hearts find our rest, and our bodies find our home in our God.

Many years ago, I was a restless young man who was in search of fame and fortune. After many years of working and wheeling, I found I had enough power, position and possession to live a life of pleasure and convenience. But my activities in the secular world brought me no peace and quiet; no fulfillment or sense of purpose. I found no answer to the questions that hounded me: Who am I? Why was I born? Where am I headed for?

It was only when I committed myself to the Father; when I acknowledged the loving presence of the Holy Spirit in my life; when I proclaimed Jesus my Lord and Savior, Healer and Friend, when I began to imitate and follow His word and His ways; when I learned that my purpose in life was to obey and glorify Him; when I realized that though I was in this world, I did not belong to this world; that though I was a resident of earth, I was truly a citizen of heaven. It was only when I commended to the Father all that I had been, all that I was and all the best that I could ever be — it was then that I found the meaning and purpose of life. I had to die to own pride, my own private ambitions, my own human aspirations to be able to live, to rise and to resurrect into a new life on high with Jesus Christ.

As an offering to the Father on this Holy Friday and, in imitation of Christ, I solemnly declare that all my actions and decisions will only be in accordance with His commandments and His gospel. I hold my present office — as indeed my life and my time - by God's grace and I pledge to serve Him with fortitude, integrity and dedication. I commend to Him all my work, actions and decisions. May they be wholly acceptable to Him and bring Him glory and honor and praise!

As Jesus totally abandoned Himself to the Father on Calvary, so do I — indeed, all of us. In unwavering faith, humility in life, modesty in action and discipline in our wants, we commend our spirits, our bodies, our daily lives as a total offering and sacrifice. O, Heavenly Father, into Your hands we commend our work, our minds, our lives and our spirits. Amen.
