# Chapter 3: ‘Salamat Po’

(Editor's note: After his term as RCM president, Attorney Panganiban continued his mission of "healing the sick and giving sight to the blind."With the active support of ophthalmologist Edgardo T. Caparas, who was his vice president in RCM, and other Rotary doctors, he organized the RCM Eyebank Foundation. In this address delivered at the Manila Hotel, he thanked the various institutions and people who helped organize, finance and operate the Eyebank.)

On behalf of the RCM Eyebank Foundation and on behalf of all the blind people who will soon see the color, movement and wonders of the world, I would like to say "thank you most sincerely" to all our benefactors. I specially thank our esteemed and beloved President Fidel V. Ramos for gracing this launching of the RCM Eyebank and for dedicating his double Paul Harris contribution o f P50,000 for the exclusive use of the Eyebank. Maraming, maraming salamat po.

I also say "thank you" to the Foundation of Rotary International for its grant of $50,000. This donation was made possible through the untiring effort during the last two years of former Rotary International President Mat Caparas and former Rotary International Director Benny Santos. Our thanks also to our sister clubs in Asia, namely, the Rotary Clubs of Tokyo South, Kowloon East, Taipei, Taipei Central and Taipei Fu-Jung, as well as to the other Paul Harris fellows listed on Page 31 of the Balita- who, like President Ramos, dedicated their Paul Harris contributions to the Eyebank.

I likewise thank the local donors who gave peso contributions. They are listed on Page 32 of the Balita. Our thanks also to Rotarian Andre Simard, Canadian ambassador to the Philippines, who unfortunately is out of town today, for various laboratory equipment the Canadian government has given us.

The total contributions so far given amount to P2,735,800. Past President Santi Dumlao, who is our dreaded finemaster today, told me that the fining session a few minutes from now will round up this figure to a neat P3 million. We say "salamat po" in advance to Monsignor Santi and to all his hapless victims today!? With this sum on hand, the Eyebank is now ready to be operationalized.

I would also like to thank the many Rotarian eye doctors and surgeons who have volunteered their expertise to the Eyebank. They are led by our Foundation vice president, Dr. Ed Caparas, and his equally gifted son, Dr. Victor Caparas, as well as Dr. Benny Santos, Dr. Jack Bautista, Dr. Rado Banzon and Dr. Carling Sevilla. All these doctors are well-known ophthalmologists. They have enough patients to keep them busy all day all week. But in the name of Rotary, they have gladly cut down their leisure time to serve the poor of our country through our Eyebank. Palakpakan po natin of silang lahat.

The Rotary Club of Manila is well-known for its civic and charitable work. RCM, over the last 74 years, organized some of the best known humanitarian causes in our country, like the Philippine Tuberculosis Society, the Philippine Cancer Society, the Community Chest, the Boy Scouts of the Philippines, the RCM Medico- Surgical Missions and the Sagip Kabataan. The RCM Eyebank, which was established two years ago during my term as President of our Club, is the newest in the long line of charitable organizations founded by RCM. This is the only facility of its kind in our country, and possibly in Asean.

The Eyebank was set up to concretize the biblical gift of sight in our technological age. It aims to collect and preserve eye tissues, particularly the cornea, and to have these implanted through modern surgical techniques in the eyes of the blind or the near blind. To succeed in providing sight to the blind, the Eyebank needs at least three items: first, it needs money to buy surgical and tissue-preserving equipment; second, volunteer surgeons and ophthalmologists well-trained in implanting eye tissues; ophthalmologist-surgeons. They constitute the first and second factors necessary to make our Eyebank succeed. On the third item, I now make a plea to all of you - my fellow Rotarians and guests, as well as the general public - to make a commitment now to donate your eye tissues, particularly corneas, to take effect after your death.

In this way, even when you are gone from this life, someone will always cherish you and will pray for you because you made it possible for him to see the light of day, to enjoy the magical colors of life and to behold the wonders of this world.

As I said earlier, there are three items that Eyebank needs. You may be wondering why I was named the president of this foundation when I am not a rich philanthropist who could donate medical equipment. Neither am I a surgeon who could implant eye tissues. I am only a poor but talkative lawyer. I suspect that these doctors in the board of directors of the Eyebank Foundation led by Ed Caparas and Benny Santos conspired to elect me president because they probably thought I could produce the third item - namely, donors of eye tissues. So as a loyal Rotarian, I must show that I can produce cornea donors amongst you. And so I repeat my plea to please fill out the donors' forms in your tables and submit them to the secretariat before you leave. 4 Now, let's have a raising of hands to find out just how many kind donors there are in the Fiesta Pavilion now. As many of you who are NOT, repeat NOT, willing to donate their eyes, please stand up. There are none! I take it that all of you who did not stand up and who stayed glued to your seats are all volunteering to donate your eyes. Salamat po!

Ladies and gentlemen, a few weeks ago, President Ramos made a stirring appeal for support for his vision of our country for the year 2000. He said that by that time, the Philippines would be an NIC where average individual income would be at least $1,000, where poverty incidence would be lessened by 30 percent and where our people would be globally competitive and fully empowered to chart their destiny. Under his Philippines 2000 program, he asked our people to fight poverty, inequity and injustice. In response, let Manila Rotary's business and professional elite continue their commitment to lead the Rotary movement in the Philippines in improving the quality of life of our people, through solidarity with the poor, the weak and the afflicted. For its part and in keeping with these objectives of our president, the RCM Eyebank Foundation hereby pledges to reduce the incidence of curable blindness by at least 30 percent by the year 2000.

In closing, the RCM Eyebank Foundation reiterates its thanks to all our financial benefactors, our volunteer surgeons and our eye tissue donors - to all of you we say: MARAMING,

MARAMING SALAMAT PO.
