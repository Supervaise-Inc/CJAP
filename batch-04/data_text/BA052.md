# Love God, Serve Man -- Ch. 7: Legal Consequences of “Part-time” Service to the Government
Date:       1994-02-01
Publisher:  Philippine Daily Inquirer (ed. Isagani Yambot)
Source:     Love God, Serve Man, Philippine Daily Inquirer (ed. Isagani Yambot) — ch. 7 (batch-04 split)
By: Artemio V. Panganiban

(Editor's note: On January 21, 1994, President Fidel V. Ramos appointed Atty. Artemio V. Panganiban as member [representing the private sector] of the Presidential Commission Against Graft and Corruption. Inasmuch as Executive Order No. 151-A would allow him to serve "part-time," Attorney Panganiban decided to pose a legal query on the extent and scope of his proposed part-time office. This letter is being printed to show the author's thoroughness and care in dealing with government. Opinion No. 33, Series of 1994, of the Secretary of Justice is also published. Inasmuch as the opinion ruled that a part-time commissioner shall be subject to the same "disabilities, disallowances and disqualifications" as the full-time commissioners and not wanting to discontinue his private law practice, Attorney Panganiban respectfully declined the post.)

01 February 1994

Hon. Eufemio C. Domingo

Chairman, Presidential Commission Against Graft and Corruption

Malacañang, Manila

Dear Chairman Domingo,

This is to place in writing the constitutional and legal questions I raised regarding my appointment to the position of Commissioner in the PCAGC representing the private sector. In defining the composition of the Commission, Executive Order No. 151-A, provides, inter alia:

"Section 2. Composition - The Commission shall be composed of a Chairman and two (2) Commissioners to be appointed or designated by the President. One of the two Commissioners shall represent the private sector and may serve on a part-time basis.

"The Chairman shall have the rank, emoluments and privileges of a Presidential Assistant II. The Commissioners, on the other hand, shall have the rank, emoluments and privileges of a Presidential Assistant I." (Bold type supplied.)

On the other hand, Article VII, Section 13 of the 1987 Constitution states:

"The President, Vice-President, the Members of the Cabinet, and their deputies or assistants shall not, unless otherwise provided in this Constitution, hold any other office or employment during their tenure. They shall not, during said tenure, directly or indirectly practice any other profession, participate in any business, or be financially interested in any contract with, or in any franchise, or special privilege granted by the Government or any subdivision, agency, or instrumentality thereof, including government-owned or controlled corporations or their subsidiaries. They shall strictly avoid conflict of interest in the conduct of their office." (Bold type supplied).

More specifically, Republic Act 6713 (the Ethical Standards Law), in Sec. 7, par (b)-(2), says:

"(b) Outside employment and other activities related thereto. - Public officials and employees during their incumbency shall not: x x x "

(2) Engage in the private practice of their profession unless authorized by the Constitution or law, provided that such practice will not conflict or tend to conflict with their official functions;" (Bold type supplied.)

Obviously, the objective of the EO is to provide empowerment to the people through the appointment of a part-time Commissioner who "shall represent the private sector." Obviously also, the intention is to allow the said Commissioner to remain in the private sector, thereby enabling him/her to practice his/her profession and/ or to continue his/her business. For how else may the said Commissioner continue representing the private sector in the government on "part-time" if he is not allowed to continue his private work. I understand that in the implementation of this "people empowerment" policy, many have been appointed to commissions and committees in the executive department on a part-time basis and thus allowed to practice their professions and/or to continue with their private businesses.

However, the Constitution bars the President, Vice-President, the Members of the Cabinet, and their deputies or assistants from practicing "directly or indirectly... any other profession" or from participating "in any business." The prohibition against private practice is reiterated, this time with penal sanctions, by R.A. 6713, with a more encompassing scope, to cover all "public officials and employees."

To understand the extent of the constitutional prohibition, a threshold question may as well be: Are the Chairman and Members of the Commission covered by the words "Members of the Cabinet, their deputies and assistants."? And in the case of R.A. 6713, is the part-time Commissioner a "public official" as defined in said Act?

May I state, in this connection, that I am not unaware of a relatively recent decision (Civil Liberties Union versus The Executive Secretary, 194 SCRA 317, 1991) where the Supreme Court in a resolution dated August 1, 1991 clarified that the terms "Members of the Cabinet, their deputies or assistants… must be given their common and general acceptation as referring to the heads of the executive departments, their undersecretaries and assistant secretaries. Public officials given the rank equivalent to a Secretary, Undersecretary or Assistant Secretary are not covered by this prohibition."

However, the issue in the civil liberties case involved the prohibition against the holding "of any other office or employment during their tenure" and not squarely on the restrictions against the private practice of a profession or participation in a business enterprise. Moreover, the High Court did not exactly rule on the legality of a "part-time" office vis-a-vis the Ethical Standards law.

In summary, the questions I have are:

1) May the Commissioner who is appointed to represent the private sector and who will " serve on part-time basis" legally "practice any other profession" or "participate in any business" while concurrently exercising his part-time duties in the Commission? Specifically, in my case, will I be allowed to practice my law profession as Senior Partner in the Panganiban Benitez Parlade Africa and Barinaga law partnership and/or to continue as an officer/director of business firms (including directorships in the Baron Travel Corporation and the Philippine Daily Inquirer, Inc.) without violating the Constitution and the laws of the land?

2) Is the part-time Commissioner subject to the "Statements and Disclosure" provisions of our laws, particularly Sec. 8 of the said RA 6713 as well as other pertinent laws on the subject?

Mr. Chairman, I believe that these questions should be clarified by the proper officials of our government, like an Opinion of the Secretary of Justice, before I take my oath and assume my position as part-time Commissioner representing the private sector. I know that many other private individuals have assumed their offices as part-time commissioners or committee members in executive bodies/groups and continued to practice their private professions or businesses. But I believe that the PCAGC - being the executive entity created to set the example of faithfully following our anti-graft laws and thoroughly investigating those who violate them - should put these questions at rest through a definitive ruling, lest its acts (and my own as a member of the Commission) be subject to attack or to doubt. At the very least, actions done in good faith in accordance with such opinion or ruling will not subject us to any sanction provided under the Ethical Standards law (Cf. SEC. 10-(b), R.A. 6713).

Thank you.

Very truly yours,

ARTEMIO V. PANGANIBAN

Senior Partner

Panganiban Benitez Parlade Africa

and Barinaga Law Offices
