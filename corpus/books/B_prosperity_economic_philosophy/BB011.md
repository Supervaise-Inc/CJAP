# Love God, Serve Man -- Ch. 14: Investment Prospects in Philippine Tourism

(Editor's note: Among the "instant" businesses emphasized by the government of President Corazon C. Aquino was tourism. In 1988, Taiwanese businessmen visited the country and wanted to assess investment possibilities in tourism. Atty. Artemio V. Panganiban, though retired even then as a part-time tourism entrepreneur, was asked by the Philippine-Chinese Business Council to brief the visitors. The author responded with this speech delivered on February 28, 1988 at the Manila Garden Hotel in Makati, Metro Manila.)

Introduction The year 1988 was a banner year of economic achievements for the Philippines.

The economy was brisk and indicators were that it was on its way to recovery. The towering performance is reflected in the 7 percent growth in real gross national product (GNP), higher than the 5.7 percent target for the year.

The investment climate in the Philippines is attractive because of many reasons, among which are:

1. Businessmen, both local and foreign, have confidence in the political and business climate;

2. The government offers attractive incentives to investors;

3. The peace and order situation in the country has stabilized.

Confidence of Businessmen Both Filipino and foreign businessmen have seen the potentials of investing once more in the Philippines. This confidence of the investors can be gleaned from figures from the Board of Investments (BOI). Statistics from the BOI show that P30.97 billion ($1.475 billion) was invested during the year 1988. This figure is 160 percent more than the P11.9 billion in 1987, and exceeds the P21-billion ($1 billion) target for 1988 by 47.5 percent.

The foreign investments in 1988 eclipsed by 70.1 percent the total foreign capital infusion of P11.9 billion in 1987 and was almost three times the P885.4 million recorded in 1986.

In the tourism industry, the biggest investment news is the infusion of some $80 million by Singapore-based Shangri-la Hotel Corporation, which is set to build a 600-room, 20-story hotel on Ortigas in Mandaluyong, and a still bigger one in Ayala Commercial Center in the heart of Makati.

In 1988, the Japanese were the top investors here, followed by the British (represented largely by Hong Kong interest) and then the Americans.

Investment activity is expected to remain upbeat. The appreciation of currencies in Asia has made production costs there less competitive, prompting investors to go overseas in search of cheap manufacturing costs. Also, in a recently concluded Philippine Investors' Forum, it was the consensus of foreign attendees to push through with 71 projects worth $234 million in 1989 and 1990. Pluses for the Investors The 1988 Investment Priorities Plan (IPP) identifies 277 areas of economic activity that are to be entitled to investment incentives under the Omnibus Investments Code of 1987. As the

Secretary of Trade and Industry, Jose S. Concepcion Jr., describes the IPP, it is "the vehicle to direct investments into those areas where the country's need is greatest." The IPP was devised to give further substance to the overall thrust of the BOI towards a promotional, rather than regulatory, regime in investments.

The Omnibus Investment Code of 1987 The Omnibus Investment Code of 1987 is a compilation of the foreign investment laws and various incentive schemes administered by the Department of Trade and Industry. The most significant features of this new code is the alignment of the Philippine incentive schemes to that of other Asean countries. The other main features are: (1) income tax holiday for enterprises engaged in a preferred area of investment; (2) new incentive for labor-intensive enterprises in the form of tax deduction for additional direct labor expenses; (3) private sector representation in the board of governors of the BOI.

Investors in tourism enterprises, provided they are registered with the BOI, shall be entitled to the following fiscal incentives: (1) income tax holiday of four years (from commercial operation) for non-pioneer and six years for pioneer firms, (extended for another year in some cases); (2) tax and duty-free importation of capital equipment, machinery and accompanying spare parts; (3) tax credit on domestic capital equipment for the same period and under the same conditions as provided in the preceding paragraph; (4) additional deduction for labor expense; (5) access to bonded manufacturing/trading warehouse system; and (6) exemption from contractor's tax, both on the national and local levels.

The investors will likewise be entitled to the following nonfiscal incentives: (1) simplification of customs procedures; (2) unrestricted use of consigned equipment (provided a re-export bond is posted unless equipment and spare parts have been imported tax

and duty-free); (3) employment of foreign nationals in supervisory, technical or advisory positions.

Also, those who venture into projects located in less developed areas will be automatically entitled to the incentives provided for a pioneer registered enterprise. The cost of necessary and major infrastructure and public utilities constructed may be deducted from taxable income 100 percent.

Lastly, under Executive Order No. 63, any alien who invests the amount of at least US$50,000 in a tourist-related project or in any tourist establishment shall be entitled to the following benefits and incentives:

1. The investor, his wife and unmarried minor children shall be permitted to enter and reside in the Philippines for as long as the investment subsists.

2. The investor shall have the right to remit earnings from the investment in the currency in which the investment was originally made (and at the exchange rate prevailing at the time of the remittance).

3. The investor shall have the right to repatriate the entire proceeds of the liquidation of the investments in the currency in which the investment was originally made and at the currency in which originally made (and at the exchange rate prevailing at the time of repatriation).

4. In case of death, the surviving immediate family shall be entitled to the same privileges.

## Philippine Tourism

During the year just past, the country hit the one-million mark in visitor arrivals, zooming past the 860,000 target for 1988.

Tourism Secretary Jose Antonio Gonzalez estimated this to translate into over $1.5 billion in foreign exchange earnings for the year. The DOT targets 1.2 million tourists in 1989 and 1.5 million in

1991. President Corazon C. Aquino is personally interested in the development of tourism and personally bats for a stronger and more creative tourism program. In response, the Department of Tourism devised its Fiesta Island Program, a five-year promotional program to start in 1989, that is geared toward attracting not only foreign but local tourists as well. Spurred by more employment opportunities and increased wages, more Filipinos are forecast to be able to afford to travel and see their own country. The department is also pushing its Balik Fiesta Program. This program, an improved version of its Balik Bayan Program, is designed to tap overseas Filipinos to revisit their home country. Similarly, American veterans will be invited to revisit World War II shrines and landmarks in the country. Toward this end, Proclamation No. 130 designates the period July 1987 to June 1992 as Pacific War Veterans Homecoming Period. The Philippines is also being positioned as an incentive destination. Invitational travel programs have been issued to decision makers to give them a first-hand chance to experience the convention facilities of the country. The Philippines is blessed with some of the world's most spectacular natural beauty. Its naturally hospitable people entice visitors to return to the Philippines time and time again. (The DOT reports that almost 60 percent of the total tourists in 1988 were repeat visitors.) Our people smile often and easily. As one writer of a prestigious American newsmagazine commented, "You will see more smiles in a single week in the Philippines than in one year in New York. Their gentle, old-fashioned concern for the traveler makes a holiday in the Philippines an incredibly enriching experience!"

Lastly, tourists will come to the Philippines because they can have more for less here. Indeed, the Philippines is the best bargain in Asia.

To accommodate the more than two million tourists targeted by 1991, the country needs 7,000 new de luxe hotel rooms, 1,300 new first-class hotel rooms and 3,000 standard rooms, 1,000 new rooms in tourist inns, 1,000 rooms in apartelles, 700 in pensions and 12,245 more in resort hotels spread throughout the various geographical divisions of the country. A total of 1,400 rental cars and 1,144 coach seats and a total of 521,102 additional airline seats (per year) will also be required. These new facilities need $300 million worth of new investments, a DOT official said.

Tourism is listed under the IPP which classifies a new deluxe hotel, like Shangri-la Hotel, as a pioneer industry. Investors in a pioneer industry are allowed incentives exclusive to their like.

The Tourism Investment Priorities Plan, an off-shoot of the IPP, has particularly urged the government to allow existing tourist transport operators to modernize their fleet to a maximum of 50 percent through duty-free importation for replacement units. Existing tour transport operators are qualified to import bus units taxfree.

The DOT has also requested the BOI to provide fiscal incentives to enterprises which will purchase and operate air transport units like light planes and helicopters.

## Peace and Order in the Philippines

Complementing the favorable business climate is the improved peace and order situation in the country.

The Department of National Defense reported achieving six "firsts" in the year 1988, namely:

1. It captured the most number of CPP/NPA/NDF leaders since 1976;

2. It arrested the growth of NP A affected areas (barangays);

3. It halted the growth of NPA regular strength;

4. It reversed the combat fatalities ratio in its favor;

5. It reversed firearms losses ratio, and

6. It reversed the tactical initiative (encounters) ratio.

## Actual Investment Prospects

in the Tourism Industry The sale of government-owned hotels offers another attraction for investors. Buying an existing hotel costs much less than putting up a new one. Still to be auctioned off are Manila Hotel and Philippine Plaza Hotel, both de luxe hotels in Metro Manila, and Puerto Azul, a resort hotel just outside the metropolis.

There are other pieces of property owned by government for sale or for lease.

Some of them are the following:

1. Fort Ilocandia Resort Hotel - Situated in Laoag, Ilocos Norte, 10 minutes away from the Laoag International Airport, seven to eight hours by land transport from Manila. Facilities include de luxe air-conditioned rooms, a 200 to 800 capacity ballroom, and the usual amenities of a de luxe resort hotel.

2. Paoay Lake Golf and Sports Complex - Also in Ilocos Norte, 20 minutes away from Laoag International Airport. Facilities include hotel rooms and sports facilities like an 18- hole golf course, a volleyball court, tennis court.

3. Banaue Hotel and Youth Hotel - Set against the magnificent view of the Banaue Rice Terraces, it is in Banaue, Ifugao province, about eight hours away from Baguio City by car or by bus.

4. Mt. Data Hotel - Also in Mt. Province, it is a fourhour drive from Baguio City and another four-hour travel via the Halsema Mountain trail. Mt. Data is 2,256 meters above sea level. Facilities include first class cozy rooms and a lobby with a display of artifacts of the different mountain tribes.

5. Marbella Marina Beach Resort Hotel - Situated at the mouth of Manila Bay, about 90 minutes away from the center of Manila by car. Facilities include condominium buildings, de luxe rooms and suites, a beach club, private beach, private heliport and a marina.

6. Leyte Park Hotel - A five-star hotel in Tacloban City in Leyte. It is 1 hour 20 minutes away from Manila by plane. Its six-hectare domain includes the usual amenities of a five-star hotel like de luxe rooms and cottages, restaurants, function rooms, swimming pool and private beach. Indeed, the time is ripe to make an investment in Philippine tourism. After many years of decline covering the years 1981 to 1985, tourism has shown an unmistakable upswing in arrivals, investments and returns. Unlike many industries, tourism offers not only attractive investment opportunities in both the long and short

terms, but a very short incubation period for profits to be realized. The wise entrepreneur will not wait for tomorrow. He will invest NOW.

I thank you.

## Summary

Chapter 14 of 'Love God, Serve Man' is the briefing CJP gave at the Manila Garden Hotel, at the request of the Philippine-Chinese Business Council, to Taiwanese businessmen assessing tourism investments in the Philippines. He opened by calling 1988 'a banner year of economic achievements,' citing 7 percent real GNP growth against a 5.7 percent target, and gave three reasons the climate was attractive: investor confidence, government incentives, and a stabilized peace and order situation. Board of Investments figures carried the first point -- P30.97 billion invested in 1988, 160 percent above 1987 and 47.5 percent past target, with foreign capital nearly triple the 1986 level, the Japanese leading, and Singapore-based Shangri-la committing some $80 million to new hotels in Mandaluyong and Makati. For incentives he walked through the 1988 Investment Priorities Plan's 277 preferred areas, described by Secretary Jose S. Concepcion Jr. as 'the vehicle to direct investments into those areas where the country's need is greatest'; the Omnibus Investments Code of 1987, aligned with ASEAN practice; six fiscal and three non-fiscal incentives for BOI-registered tourism enterprises; automatic pioneer status for less developed areas; and Executive Order No. 63, which gives any alien investing US$50,000 or more residence for his family, remittance of earnings and repatriation of proceeds. He then reported tourism's own numbers -- the first million arrivals, over $1.5 billion in foreign exchange, targets of 1.2 million for 1989 and 1.5 million for 1991, President Aquino's personal interest, and the DOT's Fiesta Island and Balik Fiesta programs -- and quantified the shortfall of rooms, rental cars, coach and airline seats requiring roughly $300 million in new investment. After citing the defense department's six 'firsts' against the insurgency, he listed the government hotels and resorts still for sale or lease, from Manila Hotel to Leyte Park, and closed that the wise entrepreneur would not wait for tomorrow but invest now.
