# Love God, Serve Man -- Ch. 2: A Year of Love and Service

(Editor's note: In his valedictory as president of the Rotary Club of Manila on June 27, 1991, Attorney Panganiban reported before a standing room crowd at the Manila Hotel Fiesta Pavilion how his theme of "Love God, Serve Man" was implemented during his one-year tenure and how RCM members worked together in faith to "help those who cannot help themselves and... who cannot help us back in return." This speech was printed in toto in the June 28 and 30, 1991 issues of the Philippine Star and the July 1, 1991 issue of the Manila Bulletin.)

One year ago, I stood before you on this very rostrum, awed by the heavy responsibilities placed upon my lean and unworthy shoulders by the presidency of the oldest and largest civic club in Asia. I knew then that I was stepping into the shoes worn by great and honorable men ahead of me. I knew then that I was tasked to lead a venerable organization with a long history of unparalleled achievements. I knew then that the Rotary Club of Manila had a well-earned reputation for excellence in all its endeavors. And I knew too that I could not steer it to anything less.

## New Rotary Theme

One year ago, I spelled out my vision of Rotary, my statement of mission. I boldly proclaimed a new theme, LOVE GOD, SERVE MAN. I said that Manila Rotary was to have a three-point vision. Like a triad and a pyramid, it would stand for three things. First, it would serve its members, its colleagues in other Clubs and its friends. Second, it would aim for excellence in the professions, in business, in education and in government. Third, Manila Rotary would work mostly, mainly and fervently, for the poor, the afflicted and the weak. In my inaugural address, I emphasized that this third element "is the base, the most important component, the driving force of our vision, our triad and our pyramid... To me, this is the raison d'etre, the reason for being of Manila Rotary — to help those who cannot help themselves and to help those who cannot help us back in return."

One year ago, during our induction, I distributed to all of you a printed 72-page Program of Action which described in detail our projects and programs designed to implement this credo of LOVE GOD, SERVE MAN. It also discussed how our Club and our Committees planned to accomplish them during specific dates in our one year of incumbency. Though we planned 178 projects, we added seven more during the year for a total of 185, and though we organized 67 committees at the start, we formed five more later for a total of 72 all in all.

Today, just after this address, we will distribute to all of you another document, an even thicker printed 94-page Annual Report which describes the RCM projects which were actually implemented. Every accomplishment of our Club, as of a week ago, is recorded for posterity in this piece of work, a fitting memorial to those who toiled and carried on the highest traditions of loving God and serving man. These achievements are not mine. They properly belong to the indefatigable Rotarians of our Club, particularly our directors and officers as well as our committee chairmen and cochairmen. Yes, we Manila Rotarians rose to the exacting demands of "SERVICE ABOVE SELF" and once more outdid ourselves in serving others - in three general ways.

## Continuation of Traditional Projects

First, we tirelessly continued our traditional projects like:

• Our medico-surgical missions, one of which was held for the first time in Malacañang;

• Our eye clinic and outreach program;

• Our free health and nutrition education campaign;

• Our anti-drug abuse project;

• Our newsboy "Mini-Olympics";

• Our shoeshine stand;

• Our essay-writing contest;

• Our teacher development and value formation program;

• Our "Voice of Rotary" national oratorical contest;

• Our journalism awards;

• Our employer-employee relations activities;

• Our vocational enrichment programs;

• Our career guidance projects;

• Our Thursday meetings which were graced by outstanding personalities of our country;

• Our weekly Balita;

• Our regular fellowship and social activities;

• and many, many more.

## Improvement of Existing Programs

Second, we expanded and improved existing projects, like televising for two and a half hours our Metro Manila Choral Festival; including short-term hands-on computer courses in our scholarship program; securing donations of bakery equipment and mini-bus for our Sagip Kabataan Center; enlarging the coverage of our Rotary Village Corps from 4 to 13 barangays in San Andres Bukid, Manila; eradicating polio in 123 barangays in Tondo, Manila through the immunization of 20,000 babies there; getting a record number of 14 Paul Harris fellows to donate $1,000 each to the Rotary Foundation; and undertaking a massive public relations campaign in all media outlets that one advertising agency conservatively estimated to be worth at least P7 million.

## Launching of Six New Projects

Third, we launched or undertook six new projects, namely, the Four-Way Test posters, the "Ang Katotohanan" program; the Eye Bank, DISCON 91, the "Preserve Planet Earth" information blitz, and the Earthquake Relief and Rehabilitation program. These six new projects deserve some more comments.

• We printed and distributed 100,000 posters showing the Rotary Four-way Test in English with simultaneous translations in one of eight Filipino dialects. These were distributed and displayed in public places all over the Philippines, including barangay centers and sari-sari stores. These created a new awareness of Rotary in the countryside and its mission of promoting truth, fairness, goodwill and beneficiality.

• Our "Ang Katotohanan" project complemented the four-way test campaign by exposing and curbing the evils of lies and distortions, particularly in official proceedings. This project includes the drafting of laws to prevent, punish and prosecute perjury and falsehoods more effectively.

• Our new Eye Bank, as of now, has already $20,000 in donations - $10,000 each from RC of Tokyo South and RC Kowloon East. A grant of $50,000 from the RI Foundation is being finalized. And just a few days ago we received the first response for the local counterpart of P 100,000 from Citibank.

• The RCM-sponsored DISCON '91 was dubbed as "the most successful ever" by Rotary International with a record-breaking registration of 2,695. All 62 Clubs in our district participated, with 17 of them registering 100 percent of their members.

• Regarding our "Preserve Planet Earth" information blitz, who has not heard of our Tagalog radio commercials? Who has not viewed our ecology spiel on television? Who has not read our ads which were printed in all major national dailies, and even international publications? Considering the circulation, readership, broadcast range, listenership and viewership of media used, it is estimated that at least 75 percent of the literate population of

• the country was exposed to one or more of the different campaign materials.

• During our term, RCM responded with love and service during three major disasters: the killer earthquake in July 1990, the devastating typhoon in September 1990 and the eruption of Mt. Pinatubo in June 1991. Everyone remembers July 16, 1990. On that day, the most destructive earthquake in Philippine history struck and devastated Central and Northern Luzon. In characteristic unity, the Rotarians of Manila rose to the challenge of our new theme of LOVE GOD, SERVE MAN. Almost everyone wrote checks. But more than just contributing money, Rotarians rolled up their sleeves and waded deep into personal "hands-on" service. Medical missions were dispatched. Food, medicines and clothing were distributed. A Tent City to house 250 families temporarily was put up in Burnham Park. Contributions were given to the Red Cross and to the Miners' Fund. But RCM was not satisfied with temporary relief. It also provided for long-term needs, particularly for school children who were deprived of their classrooms. Last February, RCM repaired the Juan Luna Elementary School in Manila. And a week ago, RCM turned over seven new school buildings to the Doña Aurora Elementary School and Quezon Elementary School in Baguio, in time for the school opening. Last September, we provided relief to the victims of typhoon "Ruping."

And even as we are holding this Valedictory in the plush Manila Hotel, our relief team is busy attending to the homeless victims of Mt. Pinatubo.

## RCM Attained 95% of Its Goals

Ladies and gentlemen, at this Valedictory meeting, the question to be answered is: How did we measure up to our theme of LOVE GOD, SERVE MAN? In other words, how successful were we during the year?

One perceptive analyst said that the success of an organization can be measured according to four criteria, namely: One, the attainment of its self-imposed goals through the implementation of its planned activities and projects. (GOALS ATTAINED)

Two, the "hands-on" participation of its members in these activities and projects. (MEMBERS? PARTICIPATION)

Three, the financial support it generated (FINANCIAL SUPPORT), and Four, the accolade, awards and recognition given by the public. (AWARDS RECEIVED)

As to the first criterion, namely, GOALS ATTAINED, I ask you to compare the Program of Action with our Annual Report and you will discover that about 95 percent of the projects and activities that we planned in our Program of Action were accomplished or at least worked on. Aside from those described in our PoA, we had activities, like the Earthquake Relief and Rehabilitation, that were not planned. But the indomitable Rotarians of Manila innovated, adapted and succeeded even in these unplanned projects.

As to the second criterion, namely, MEMBERS' PARTICIPATION, our Annual Report will show that all 72 of our Committees met - some as often as weekly, some occasionally - but all tried relentlessly to implement their plans. A great many of us were united in mind and spirit as we traveled the highways and byways of our long-established projects. Many more scaled greater heights of excellence as we expanded and enlarged the already successful programs. Still many, many more linked arms in solidarity and partnership as we joyfully attended to the needs of the poor, the afflicted and the weak.

## Financial Support

As to the third criterion, namely, FINANCIAL SUPPORT, P22 million, in cash and in kind, were raised and expended during this year. Anyway we look at it, this is a staggering amount. (I am told that the highest budget of any previous Rotary year was not more than P5 million.) This gigantic outlay funded all our projects and activities. For our top three projects alone, P15 million was raised and spent as follows: P8 million for the Earthquake fund, P4 million for the "Preserve Planet Earth" campaign and P3 million for DISCON ‘91.

As for the fourth criterion (AWARDS RECEIVED), RCM to date has received several awards, both nationally and internationally, both from the government and the private sector, both from Rotary International and from non-Rotary organizations. Let me name some of them:

## Awards and Citations

We were given two "Best Public Service" trophies by the Catholic Mass Media Awards, a rare Silver Jubilee "Community Service" Plate by the Legal Management Council of the Philippines, the "Best Mid-Year Presentation" trophy and other trophies by Rotary International, the "Outstanding Manilan in Civic Leadership Award" by the City of Manila and the "Gold Quill" by the US-based International Association of Business Communicators.

In addition, two of the most widely circulated national magazines, the Sunday Panorama of the Manila Bulletin and the Starweek of the Philippines Star, carried a month ago full-blown articles applauding RCM and its work for the poor, the weak and the afflicted.

Though some of these awards and citations, like the 1991 "Best Manilan" award, were given to me personally, I feel I could not have been worthy of them were it not for RCM's monumental achievements in civic work.

## Thank You for Rewarding Me with a Glorious Year of Achievements

Ladies and Gentlemen, today, on my valedictory, our Club shall officially put to a close our theme of LOVE GOD, SERVE MAN. I thank you, my fellow Rotarians, for the glorious year that you have rewarded me with. I thank you for your joy, for your service, for your comradeship, for your civic-mindedness, for your selflessness. But most of all, I thank you for your love. Your love that is inseparably love for God and love for man. I thank you for your love when I was angry, when I was stubborn, when I was late, when I was proud, when I was wrong.

It is praiseworthy to serve our fellowmen, to achieve our self-imposed goals, to garner awards and human accolades. It is heavenly to feed the hungry, clothe the naked, heal the sick and shelter the homeless. But as St. Paul declares (1 Cor. 13):

"If I speak with human and angelic tongues but do not have love, I am a noisy gong, a clanging cymbal... if I have faith great enough to move mountains, but have no love, I gain nothing.. if I give everything I have to feed the poor and hand over my body to be burned but have no love, I am nothing."

Love, after all, is what enables us to know God and to be like Him. "For," as Scripture says, "God is Love." (1 John 4:8).

## May the Rotary Tribe Ever Increase

Truly, Rotary Year 1990-1991 was a memorable year of achievements, of honors, of personal fulfillment, of service and of love. Though there were harried days, momentary days of conflict, tension, deadlines and near-misses - in the over-all, it has been a grand year of opportunity to be creative, to be innovative, to be patient, "to announce a year of favor" (Luke 4:19) and to give of myself as a Rotarian for others and as a man for God. For all these and more, I thank all of you, my fellow Rotarians, my brothers. May your tribe ever increase. May your zeal, your faith and your love ever grow.

Thank you.

## Summary

Chapter 2 of 'Love God, Serve Man' is CJP's valedictory as president of the Rotary Club of Manila, delivered on June 27, 1991 at the Manila Hotel Fiesta Pavilion, one year after he had stood on the same rostrum awed by the heavy responsibilities placed upon his lean and unworthy shoulders. He restates the three-point vision he had boldly proclaimed under the theme LOVE GOD, SERVE MAN -- members and colleagues, excellence in the professions and government, and mostly the poor, the afflicted and the weak -- and then sets the year's promises against its record: a 72-page Program of Action with 178 projects and 67 committees had grown to 185 projects and 72 committees, and was answered by a 94-page Annual Report distributed that evening. The achievements, he insists, are not his. He accounts for them in three parts: traditional projects continued, from medico-surgical missions to the newsboys' Mini-Olympics; existing programs expanded, including polio immunization for 20,000 babies in Tondo and the enlargement of the Rotary Village Corps from 4 to 13 barangays; and six new projects -- the Four-Way Test posters in eight dialects, Ang Katotohanan, the Eye Bank, DISCON '91 with a record 2,695 registrants, the Preserve Planet Earth media blitz, and the Earthquake Relief and Rehabilitation program born of three disasters, the July 16, 1990 earthquake, typhoon Ruping and the eruption of Mt. Pinatubo whose victims the Club's relief team was attending to even as he spoke. He then measures the year against four criteria: about 95 percent of goals attained, all 72 committees active, P22 million raised and spent against a previous high of P5 million, and awards from the Catholic Mass Media Awards to the Gold Quill, personal honors included that he says he could not have deserved without the Club. He closes by thanking the Rotarians for a love that is inseparably love for God and love for man, quoting St. Paul that without love he would be a noisy gong, and blessing their zeal, faith and love.
