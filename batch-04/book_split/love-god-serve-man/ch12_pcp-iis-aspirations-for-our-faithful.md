# Chapter 12: PCP II's Aspirations for Our Faithful

(Editor's note: Like the preceding speech, this talk was meant to bring the message of PCP II to the Catholic faithful. The difference is that this was delivered before the masses - the people of Smokey Mountain who gathered 5,000 - strong at the Folk Arts Theater as the General Assembly of the Sambayanang Kristiano on May 19, 1991. Hence, the reader will notice the difference in style and language. The following is a transcript of an extemporaneous talk - which explains the rather repetitive style.)

## Introduction

Nagpapasalamat po ako sa mga namumuno ng Sambayanang Kristiano ng Banal na Diwa sa kanilang paanyaya sa akin dito sa pangkalahatang pagsasama-sama ninyo. Salamat po kay Pangulong Edmar A. Jimenez, Uma-aktong Pangulong Estelita Bianes Pangkalahatang Kalihim na si Luzviminda Salcedo, at higit sa lahat, sa Pangkalahatang Pang-ispiritual na Patnugot na si Fr. Ben Beltran. Hiniling po nila na ako raw ay magsalita sa inyo tungkol sa Second Plenary Council of the Philippines o Ikalawang Pangkalahatang Konseho Ng Pilipinas.

Ngunit bago po ako magpatuloy ng aking kaunting nalalaman sa bagay na ito, sa wari ko po ay nararapat lamang na magkakilala tayo, hindi lamang kung sino ako o sino kayo sapagkat yan po ay alam na nating lahat. Tayo pong lahat ay anak ng Diyos. Hindi po ba? Ngunit ang gusto ko pong malaman lamang ay ang mga iniisip at mga kuru-kuro ninyo sa mga bagay-bagay, lalunglalo na sa ating pagka Kristiyano. Maitanong ko lamang sa inyo, alam po ba ninyo ang theme song ng mga taong nasa impiyerno?

Ang theme song po ng mga taong nasa impierno ay "SANA." Habang nasa impierno sila ang kanilang sinasabi ay, SANA noong ako'y nabubuhay pa minahal ko ang aking asawa at ang aking pamilya. SANA noong ako'y nabubuhay pa sa mundo minahal ko ang aking kapitbahay at kapwa-tao. SANA, noong ako 'y nabubuhay pa ako'y nag-miembro sa Sambayanang Kristiano ng Banal na Diwa upang aking nalaman at natuntunan ang pagmamahal ni Hesu Kristo. SANA, noong ako 'y nabubuhay pa sa mundo tumulong ako sa kapwa ko; nagpakatino ako at sinunod ko ang lahat ng sinabi ni Hesu Kristo. Iyan po ang theme song ng mga taong nasa impierno. "SANA".

Ngunit para sa kanila, huli na ang lahat, tapos na ang lahat at hindi na nila mababago pa ang kanilang paghihinagpis at pagkadarang sa impierno.

Ngayon pong alam na natin ang theme song ng mga nasa impierno, maitanong ko naman sa inyo; alam ba naman po ninyo ang theme song ng mga taong nasa langit?

Opo. Ang theme song ng mga taong nasa langit ay "Osana". At sinisigaw nila araw-araw, gabi-gabi: "Osana sa Diyos Ama! Osana sa Diyos Anak! Osana sa Espiritu Santo!" Araw-araw po yan ang sinisigaw nila sapagkat sila ay nasa piling ng Diyos at sila 'y masaya. Osana! Osana! Iyan po ang sigaw at kanta nila.

Ngayong alam na ninyo ang theme song ng mga nasa impierno at ang theme song ng mga nasa langit, ano po ang pagkakaiba nito? Ang pagkakaiba po ay: "O". Ano po ang ibig sabihin ng "O"? Ang ibig pong sabihin ng "O" ay "Ostia, Ostia." Iyan pong Ostia na tuwing magmimisa ay nagiging katawan ni Kristo. Sapagkat ang Ostia ay talagang si Kristo. At tuwing tatanggapin po natin ang Ostiang iyan na katawan ni Kristo tayo po ay napapalapit sa langit at napapakanta rin ng "Osana!"

Ngayon po na nagkakaintindihan na tayo sa mga theme

song, ay pumunta naman tayo sa layunin ng ating pagtitipon-tipon ngayon. Gaya po ng nasabi ko na, hiniling ni Father Ben at ni Brother Edmar at Sister Luzviminda at Sister Estelita na magsalita po ako tungkol sa Plenary Council.

Alam po ninyo yang Second Plenary Council of the Philippines ay naganap noong ika-20 ng Enero hanggang ika-20 ng Pebrero 1991 sa San Carlos Pastoral Complex. Samakatuwid, yan po ay naganap ng halos isang buwan. Araw-araw po kaming nagpupulong. Nag-umpisa ang mga araw namin ng ika-anim at kalahati ng umaga at nagtapos ng ika-anim ng gabi. Kaya't ipagpaumanhin ninyo na hindi ko masasabi sa inyo ang lahat nang nangyari sa Plenary Council.

Ngunit sa araw na ito, bayaan ninyo na sabihin ko sa inyo ang tatlong bagay. Una, ano ba ang ibig sabihin ng isang Plenary Council? Pangalawa, ano po ba ang dalawang napagkayarian sa PCP II na kailangang malaman natin sa ating pagsasama-sama ngayon? At, pangatlo, papano po nating maisabuhay ang mga napagkayarian na yan sa Plenary Council?

## Three Items for Discussion

I intend to discuss three things with you today. First, what is the nature and function of a Plenary Council? Second, what are the two salient conclusions or aspirations of the Second Plenary Council of the Philippines? And three, how can we live these conclusions or aspirations of PCP II?

A Plenary Council is a gathering of bishops and other ecclesiastical authorities in a number of ecclesiastical areas, oftentimes of a whole country, for the purpose of making new laws and renewing the faith of our people. When the decrees or agreements or policies made by a Plenary Council are approved by the Holy See, they become part of church law for that jurisdiction.

We are talking about the Second Plenary Council. This implies that there must have been a first. The first Plenary Council in the Philippines was held in 1953. It was attended by bishops and priests only. At that time, the population of the Philippines was only 27 million compared to the 60 million we have now.

## Nature of PCP II

The Second Plenary Council of the Philippines, otherwise

referred to as PCP II, was convened by the Pope at the instance of the Catholic Bishops Conference of the Philippines (CBCP). It was attended by 489 participants composed of 93 archbishops and bishops, 226 priests, 14 religious sisters and brothers and 156 lay people. PCP II is significant in the sense that this is the first time in the 2,000-year history of the Catholic Church in the world, not just in the Philippines, that laymen were invited to participate in the law-making of the Church. I am both honored and humbled by the thought that God has made it possible for me to participate in this historic gathering of the leaders of our Church. For truth to tell, I felt very unworthy even after I have received the invitation or "summons" to participate in the Council. In the beginning, I was very cautious and I did not want to speak up because I felt that I was not adequately trained to be able to discuss the theology, the "whats" and "the whys" of our faith with the best minds of our Church. However, through the encouragement of my co-participants, particularly the bishops and archbishops, I gradually took courage. And with the help of prayers to the Holy Spirit, I later on even debated with the other participants. It was a very enlightening, a very ennobling experience that I will never forget in my life.

As I said earlier, the council met for more than one month. Thus it is physically impossible to explain all the things that we discussed in that meeting in the just 30 minutes that I was given to speak today. However, I would like to give you two aspirations of PCP II which I consider very important and relevant in our lives. First, the Plenary Council of the Philippines called for the RENEWAL of the life of the faithful and second, the Plenary Council called for the SOCIAL TRANSFORMATION of our church into the "Church of the Poor."

## SK's Aspirations Identifies with PCP II

Before I proceed, let me say that I thank your SK coordinator, Cris Harina, who visited me in my office last Friday, May 17,

1991. He briefed me on the nature, objectives and achievements of your organization. Looking over your brochure and your Constitution, I noticed that your aspirations - ang inyong mithin - can be summed up in two, namely:

1. "The SPIRITUAL - the cleansing of ourselves of self-centeredness and evil deeds; and,

2. "The ECONOMIC - the uplifting of our living standards through cooperatives." Let me say that these two aspirations are identical with the aspirations of PCP II, except that PCP II terms them differently as RENEWAL, for your "spiritual" and SOCIAL for your "economic."

Renewal of the Faithful Let me explain these two goals or aspirations of PCP II. First, the RENEWAL or PAGBABAGO of the faithful.

PCP II observed that our country is the only Christian country in the whole of Asia. Yet if media reports are to be believed, there is so much violence, graft and corruption, abuse of power, poverty, violation of human rights and taking of undue advantage in this Christian country of ours. As Christians, we are taught to abhor violence, to renounce graft and corruption, to avoid abuse of

power, to help the poor, the weak and the underprivileged. Why is it that, in spite of the fact that we claim to be the only Christian country in Asia, we are one of the most violent, one of the most graft-prone, one of the poorest countries in Asia? Is it because we are Christians only while we are inside the Church and attending mass and praying to our God? After we leave the Church, do we forget about our being Christians and do we become pagans and barbarians? Yes, do we become violent, do we become dishonest, do we become corruptors and corrupt?

## Conversion of Heart and Mind

The Second Plenary Council of the Philippines is advocating a conversion and a renewal of the faithful. It is asking the faithful to be Christians not only inside the Church but especially when outside of it. PCP II is asking for a conversion of the heart as well as a conversion of the mind.

A conversion of the heart requires a personal encounter with Jesus Christ. An encounter with Him on a personal basis such that your heart is moved and your spirit is touched. A conversion of the mind means that we need to be re-educated in our faith, in our belief, in our catechesis.

## Cristo-centric

A re-education of our minds and of our hearts requires four things. First, our life and our faith must be CHRIST-CENTERED. The center of our worship, the center of life, must be Jesus Christ. While it is good to have devotion to the Blessed Mother and devotions to the various saints, we must never, never forget that Christ comes first in our lives. Christ was not just a once-upon-a-time person in history but a God who is truly with us in the present, a God who truly loves and cares for us. Jesus lives!

Second, our faith and our life must be ROOTED IN THE WORD OF GOD. Our life must follow the teachings of Jesus Christ as He taught His disciples and his people two thousand years ago. This requires, therefore, a reading, an understanding, a reflection and a living of the Word of God, the Gospel, the Good News. Before the Plenary Council of the Philippines and before the Vatican II came into being, it was generally believed that our Church did not require or did not encourage the reading of the Bible. Let me make it clear to all of you that the Second Plenary Council of the Philippines is encouraging, in fact, is requiring all the faithful to read, understand, reflect on and live the Bible in our daily life.

Third, the Plenary Council of the Philippines requires our faith to be INCULTURATED in our daily life, to be authentically Filipino. That is why it encourages the use of authentic Filipino ways to be able to propagate, to be able to spread the faith. That is why it encourages the use of the Filipino language. Kaya po binabati ko kayong lahat dito sa Sambayanang Kristiano sapagkat ang ginagamit ninyo ay ang wikang Pilipino na naintindihan nating lahat. At iyan po ang sinasabi ng Plenary Council of the Philippines. Ang ating buhay Kristiyano ay dapat maging tunay na Pilipino. Hindi po maaari na ang ating pagsamba at ang ating pagmasid ay maging banyaga. Ang ating pong pagsamba at ang ating pagtingin sa Diyos ay dapat maging Pilipino. Ang ating pagsasama-sama ay Pilipino, lahing Pilipino, ugaling Pilipino. Ang ating pag-uusap at pagkakaintindihan ay sa salitang Pilipino, sa salitang nagkakaintindihan tayo, sa salitang ang diwa at kahulugan ay Pilipino.

Pang-apat, ang buhay po natin o pagsamba ay dapat "OTHERS- ORIENTED." Ang ating pong layunin ay para sa iba at hindi para sa atin lamang. A self-seeking, self-oriented faith is no faith. When our Lord Jesus Christ was asked by the Jews: "Rabbi, what is the greatest commandment of God?", Jesus replied to them: "The first commandment is to love God with all your heart, with all your mind and with all your being. Second, love your neighbor as you love yourself." Samakatuwid, ito ang sinasabi ng Diyos. Ang unang utos niya ay ang mahalin natin ang Diyos ng ating buong pag-iisip, ng ating buong puso at ng ating buong katawan. At pangalawa, mahalin natin ang ating kapwa gaya ng pagmamahal natin sa ating sarili.

Four Elements of Catechesis Kaya uulitin po natin ang sigaw ng Second Plenary Council, ano po ang pagbabagong hinihingi sa ating buhay at sa ating pagsamba? Una, tayo po ay "Christ-centered." Si Kristo po ang sentro ng ating buhay. Pangalawa, ang ating pong buhay at pagsamba ay dapat na sisiil sa Biblia, sa magandang balita. Our faith and our life must be "rooted in the word of God." Pangatlo, ang atin pong buhay at ang ating pagsamba ay nararapat na "inculturated" o nakahalo sa ating pangaraw-araw na buhay. Tayo ay Kristiano hindi lamang sa loob ng simbahan, ngunit tayo'y Kristiano rin sa ating pamamahay, sa ating hanapbuhay, sa ating pakikipagkapwa, sa ating mga kaibigan at sa ating mga kasama sa opisina at sa lahat ng taong ating nakakahalo. At pang-apat, ang ating pong buhay at pagsamba ay "others-oriented" o para sa iba at hindi para sa atin.

## Church of the Poor

Iyan po ang unang aspiration ng Second Plenary Council of the Philippines. Ang pangalawa po ay ang "SOCIAL TRANS- FORMATION." Ang gusto po ng PCP II ay maging tapat sa karamihan ng ating mga tao ng ating Simbahan. At sapagkat ang karamihan ng ating mga tao ay mahihirap, hinihingi po ng Second Plenary Council na ang ating simbahan ay maging para sa mga

mahihirap. The Second Plenary Council proclaimed our church to be a "Church of the Poor." Ang ibig po bang sabihin nito ay gusto tayong maging mahirap ng Simbahan? Hindi po. Ang ibig sabihin nito ay gusto tayong mahango sa kahirapan ng ating Simbahan - tumulong sa mga mahihirap upang sila'y mahango sa kahirapan. Hindi po sa pagbibigay ng tinatawag na "dole-out" kung hindi sa pagtuturo ng pangkabuhayan at pagkatiwasay. Ang pagsasama-sama, pagbibigkis-bigkis ng mga mahihirap - iyan po ang pangalawang layunin ng ating Simbahan ayun sa PCP II].

What is the Church of the Poor? Let me quote the language of the Second Plenary Council on the meaning of the Church of the Poor.

"A church that lives in evangelical poverty which combines detachment from material possessions with a profound trust in the Lord as the only source of salvation; "A Church that defends and vindicates the rights of the poor even when doing so spells for herself alienation and persecution by the rich and powerful; "A Church where the poor, equal to all others in Christian dignity, are not only evangelized but become evangelizers themselves;

"A Church where no one is so poor as to have nothing to give, and no one is so rich as to have nothing to receive."

Tatagalugin ko po: Isang Sambayanang hindi nagmamahal sa kayamanang materyal dahil nananalig nang buo sa Panginoon bilang tanging pinagmulan ng lahat ng kaligtasan; Isang Sambayanang nagtatanggol sa mga karapatan ng mga mahihirap kahit na ang maaaring katumbas nito ay pagtanggap sa panggigipit at paninikil ng mga mayayaman at ng may kapangyarihan;

Isang Sambayanan na kung saan ang mga nagdarahop ay katulad lang ng mga maykaya sa dignidad kaya't hindi lang sila dapat taga-tanggap ng Mabuting Balita kundi taga-pagpalaganap din nito; Isang Sambayanan na kahit na ang pinakamahirap ay mayroon pa ring maaaring maipamahagi at ang pinakamayaman ay mayroon pa ring pangangailangan.

The ideal of this "Church of The Poor" remains the first Jerusalem community, where the faithful were "united, heart and soul" (Acts 4:32) and where "no one was in want" (Acts 3:34) because everyone shared out of love. This is the kind of Church which, in all humility, PCP II hopes would emerge as the fruit.

This proclamation of our Church as the "Church of the Poor" is, according to Bro. Sonny de los Reyes, Presiding Elder of Bukas Loob Sa Diyos Charismatic Community, firstly a declaration of identity; secondly, a declaration of mission; thirdly, a declaration of constituency and, finally, a declaration of commitment. Let me explain.

## Meaning of PCP II's Proclamation

PCP II's proclamation of the CHURCH OF THE POOR is firstly a DECLARATION OF IDENTITY. Christ identified himself with the poor as shown by his teachings on the Last Judgment.

"I assure you, as often as you did it for one of my least brothers, you did it for me... I assure you, as often as you neglected to do it to one of these least ones, you neglected to do it to me." (Mat. 25:40,45)

The Church of the Poor is secondly a DECLARATION OF MISSION, as contained in the first public statement of Jesus.

"The spirit of the Lord is upon me; therefore, He has anointed me. He has sent me to bring glad tidings to the poor; to proclaim liberty to captives, recovery of sight to the blind, and release to prisoners, to announce a year of favor for the Lord." (Luke 4:18)

Christ's mission is explicitly, distinctively, the mission of reaching out to the poor to liberate them from whatever may be the cause of their poverty. The Church of Christ, if it is to be faithful to His mission, must be a Church with the purpose of healing and delivering the poor from their poverty.

The statement is thirdly, a DECLARATION OF CON- STITUENCY. This CONSTITUENCY is proclaimed in Scripture, particularly in the first public teaching of Jesus, the Sermon on the Mount:

How blessed are the poor in spirit, the reign of God is theirs." (Matt. 5:3).

In Luke 6:20, the statement is made more pointedly, more directly: "Blest are you poor, the reign of God is yours.

Finally, the message of PCP II is a DECLARATION OF

COMMITMENT, a commitment to serve the poor, to speak for them, to stand for their cause. This commitment entails an emptying of attachments to wealth and prestige, to power and privilege — not only by the Church herself as an institution but by the laity, the living members of the Church, as can be gleaned from Philippians •

"Your attitude must be that of Christ: though He was in the form of God, He did not deem equality with God something to be grasped at. Rather, He emptied

Himself and took the form of a slave, being born in the likeness of men. He was known to be of human estate." And also from 2 Corinthians 8:9: "You are well-acquainted with the favor shown you by our Lord Jesus Christ: how for your sake he made himself poor though he was rich, so that you might become rich with his poverty."

Ladies and gentlemen, as a consequence of these two main aspirations of the PCP II, let me tell you that the Plenary Council of the Philippines has changed the meaning of "Church". Formerly, "Church" refered to the concrete Church, to the place of worship where we go for mass. Sometimes also (in the past) we refered to "Church" as the hierarchy, particularly the bishops and the priests. But the Second Plenary Council of the Philippines has changed the meaning of Church. By Church, the Plenary Council of the Philippines refers to all the faithful - you and me, all of us, we are the church. So when we say that the Church in the Philippines is the church of the poor, we are not merely refering to the bishops and the priest, but to all of the faithful, to you and to me, to all the men and woman, to the adults and to the children, to the rich and to the poor alike because before God and before man, all of us are equal in dignity and in mission.

## Lay Empowerment

Because we now constitute the church, we say that the Second Plenary Council of the Philippines recognizes the "empowerment of the laity." It recognizes in simple language "people power" because it was the Church, the faithful who started people power in Mendiola, in EDSA, everywhere. There is people power here in our gathering at Folk Arts Theater. We, the people, are being called upon to lead our faith in PCP II's twin aspirations of PER- SONAL RENEWAL and SOCIAL TRANSFORMATION.

As I close this speech, let me relate to you a story I always hear from my Christian friends. They tell me: "Ever since my mind and my heart were touched by God, ever since I have started to live a Christian life, a life of love and charity, a life of mercy and compassion, I thought I would have no more problems and difficulties; no more crises and trials. But the contrary is true. In fact, I still feel the persecution, the difficulties, the pains, the sufferings of this world all the more. I thought that if I lived a good Christian life, I would be freed by God from pain, from suffering. In answer to this lament of my friends, I tell them this story related by Justice Hugo Gutierrez. You know, he said, there is a very good doctor, a specialist, who has healed a lot of people who were sick and injured. And yet he, himself, is wounded. He has a wound in his arm that would not heal, a wound that caused him so much pain, so much excruciating pain that he cannot bear it.

So one day, he went on a pilgrimage to the Holy Land. He went to the "pool of Bethesda" where our Lord Jesus Christ performed some of his greatest healing miracles. It is said that if one dips his wounded hand in the waters of the pool of Bethesda, one will get instant healing.

So this doctor, this specialist, went to the "Pool" and jostled with the crowd to wet his wounded hand. Just as he was about to place his hand in the water, a former patient of his cried out, "Doctor, doctor, please do not dip your hand into the pool."

The doctor was astonished, "Why do you not want me to be healed; why do you not want me to be rid of pain - when I healed you and rid you of your own wound?"

The patient replied, "You should retain your wound, your pain, your affliction. Because, it is only the sick that knows the struggle of the afflicted; because it is only the injured that knows the pain of the wounded. You are an effective healer because you can emphatize with the wounded."

My friends, sometimes we question the Lord. We say: Lord ako po'y mabuting Kristiano. Bakit po may sakit pa ako? Bakit po pinagsasamantalahan pa ako? Bakit po iniwan ako ng asawa ko? Well, your answer is in that little story of the doctor. Sometimes God allows us to suffer, to be wounded, to feel the pain of affliction and deprivation because He wants to use us in touching others, in healing others, in helping others. For only the injured know the pain and affliction of the wounded.

Maraming salamat po.
