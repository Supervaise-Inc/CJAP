# Reforming the Judiciary -- Ch. 1: An Introduction to the APJR
Date:       2002-12-01
Publisher:  Supreme Court Printing Press
Source:     Reforming the Judiciary, Supreme Court Printing Press — ch. 1
By: Artemio V. Panganiban

The centerpiece of the stewardship of Chief Justice Hilario G. Davide Jr., the overall head of the judicial department of our government, is the “Davide Watch” which he announced immediately after assuming office on November 30, 1998. In my book Leadership by Example which came out three years ago, I wrote extensively about this vision and mission of the Chief.

In this present volume, Reforming the Judiciary, I am focusing on the implementing effort envisioned in the Davide Watch, detailed in a 156-page document entitled “Action Program for Judicial Reform 2001-2006” (APJR).

Comprehensive, pervasive and wide-ranging, the APJR takes its bearing from reform initiatives in the past, specifically the “Blueprint of Action for the Judiciary.” It outlines the concerns and priority reforms that were identified during consultations with various stakeholders of the judiciary across the country. These consultations were held during the term of Chief Justice Andres R. Narvasa and were funded by a grant from the United Nations Development Program.

Approved by the Supreme Court on December 8, 2000, the APJR is superintended for the Court by the Executive Committee for the Judicial Reform Program. This committee is composed of Chief Justice Hilario G. Davide Jr., chairman; Justice Josue N. Bellosillo, vice chairman; and the following members: Justice Reynato S. Puno, Justice Artemio V. Panganiban, Philja Chancellor Ameurfina A. Melencio Herrera, Court Administrator Presbitero J. Velasco Jr., Asst. Clerk of Court Maria Luisa D. Villarama, Assistant Court Administrator Antonio H. Dujua, Assistant Court Administrator and Public Information Chief Ismael G. Khan Jr., Committee on Halls of Justice Chairperson Virginia A. Soriano, Management Information Systems Office Chief Ivan E. Uy, Program Management Office Director Evelyn T. Dumdum, Department of Justice Assistant Secretary Elvira Atanacio and Integrated Bar of the Philippines National President Teofilo Pilando Jr. The technical and administrative staff is composed of Noreen D. Salas, Katrina C. Monsod, John Noah Red, Nicasio O. Taneo and Estrella D. Eje.

To emphasize the importance of APJR, it is the only standing committee in the Supreme Court that is directly headed by the Chief Justice. Under his Office, a full-time Program Management Office (PMO), headed by Mrs. Evelyn Toledo-Dumdum as program director, handles its day-to-day implementation.

Due to its length, I cannot reproduce the complete text of the APJR here. However, as a fitting introduction and by way of a general background for this book, I have decided to reprint verbatim the “Executive Summary” as follows:

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

“The Judiciary has been constantly aware of the many challenges to its efficient performance; and efforts to address various concerns, though numerous, have been sporadic. But over the last two years, the Judiciary has taken concrete steps to establish a strong foundation for the long-term development of the judicial branch. One of the fundamental strides taken is the preparation of an Action Program for Judicial Reform with a wide-ranging yet comprehensive set of reform projects and activities aimed at enhancing judicial conditions and performance for the improved delivery of judicial services.

“This Action Program for Judicial Reform (APJR) is founded on the vision and mission espoused by Chief Justice Hilario G. Davide, Jr. and enunciated in the Davide Watch.

‘VISION

‘A Judiciary that is independent, effective and efficient, and worthy of public trust and confidence; and a legal profession that provides quality ethical, accessible and cost-effective legal services to our people and is willing and able to answer the call to public service.

‘MISSION

‘In pursuing the stated vision, the Davide Watch articulates the following goals: (1) delivery of speedy and fair dispensation of justice to all; (2) judicial autonomy and independence from political interference; (3) improved access to judicial and legal services; (4)  improved quality of external inputs to the judicial process; (5) efficient, effective and continuously improving judicial institutions; and (6) a judiciary that conducts its business with dignity, integrity, accountability and transparency.’

“The APJR builds on previous reform efforts by the Supreme Court, including the Technical Assistance to the Philippine Judiciary on Justice and Development Project, which produced the “Blueprint of Action for the Judiciary,” Formulation of Administrative Reforms, Review of the Criminal Justice System, Review of the Alternative Dispute Resolution Mechanism, Review of the Barangay Justice System, Assessment of the Impact of Judicial Education and Directions for Change and Development, and Formulation of a Medium-term Public Investment Program for the Judiciary. These studies established basic data providing the rationale for the proposed investments for the Judiciary, which had been identified in the “Blueprint.” These also provided the basis for the development of concrete projects and activities to realize the Judiciary’s vision.

## "1. THE REFORM FRAMEWORK

"The APJR is guided by the fundamental principles of (1) impartiality, access to and speed of judicial systems; (2) judicial autonomy and self-governance; (3) streamlined institutional structure and operations; (4) decentralization; (5) information systems-based operations, planning, performance management and decision-making; (6) competitive and equitable remuneration; (7) continuing capability improvement; (8) transparency and accountability in appointments to the Bench; and (9) consensus-building and collaboration with civil society.

"Based on these principles , a fourpronged sectoral program of reforms on judicial systems and procedures, institutions development, human resource development, and reform support systems was developed. The APJR is further translated into tangible projects primarily addressing seven (7) critical issues: case congestion and delay, budget deficiencies, politicized system for judicial appointments, lack of judicial autonomy, need the for re-engineering the human resource development system, dysfunctional administrative structure and operating systems accompanied by deficient court technologies and facilities, and the need to improve public information and collaboration with civil society. These issues cut across all reform sectors.

## "2. IMPLEMENTING THE REFORM PROGRAM

"The APJR for the period 2001-2006 requires an estimated PhP 4.3 billion in investments (Exhibit 1). Of the total financial requirements, PhP 2.305 billion or 53.45% is expected to be financed through domestic funds, while PhP 2.007 billion or 46.55% is targeted for external financing, particularly from official development assistance or ODA. Capital outlays comprise a huge portion of external financing, amounting to PhP 1.2 billion or 28.31% of the total investment program, while PhP 786 million or 18.24% of total financing will be used for technical and diagnostic studies and detailed reengineering projects.

"The biggest share (75.47%) of investment resources will go to the institution's development sector. This sector is concerned with strengthening institutional capacity through the re-engineering of high-level and downstream formal structures, clarifying functions and relationships, improving performance and administrative management systems as well as mission-critical systems and procedures, and the provisions of necessary physical and technological infrastructure and facilities to support judicial operations.

"The major projects under this sector, with a total estimated cost of PhP 3.2 billion, include: Fiscal Autonomy and Financial Resources Generation; Reengineering of Administrative Structure and Operations; Information Systems Capacity Improvement; and Construction and Provision of Infrastructure and Support Facilities.

"Human resources development projects account for the second biggest share at 18.33% of the investment program. This reform sector is concerned with internal and external human resources, which directly contribute to a properly functioning judicial system. involves the internal manpower of the judicial organization, the legal professions, and the general public which all lend to the efficient delivery of judicial services. The major projects identified in this sector are: Judicial Appointments and Career Development, Training and Development of Court Personnel, Judicial Education, Remuneration Systems Education. Reengineering, and Legal Approximately PhP 791 million in investments are required for this sector.

“Reforms in judicial systems and procedures represent 5.83% of the total investment program. This sector covers the substantive and procedural aspects of law as they affect the operating efficiency of the judicial system, particularly fairness, access, speed and the convenience with which judicial services are availed and rendered.  The major projects identified are: Reengineering of the Court Jurisdictional Structure, Strengthening of the Alternative Dispute Resolution Mechanism, Strengthening of the Barangay Justice System, Development of Judicial Performance Management System, Nationwide Development of Courts, Formulation and Institutionalization of the Judicial Research Group, and Advocacy and Collaboration for Reforms in the Other Pillars of Justice. These projects are estimated to cost PhP 251 million.

“The reform support systems sector, which will require about PhP 16 million, covers key efforts that sustain the smooth, timely and effective implementation of the entire reform program. The Supreme Court will develop and establish broad consensus among the Judiciary, the users of the courts and the rest of society concerning the need for change. The major projects identified are: Public Information for Better Transparency and Improved Access to Justice; Collaboration with Civil Society; and Review and Adoption of International Experience and Best Practice.

"The APJR anticipates that the level of external resources to finance the reform projects will start high between 2001-2002, at which time the detailed assessments, policymaking and project preparation activities are expected to be undertaken. This funding source will decline continuously over the following years as the Judiciary begins the actual implementation of projects, a substantial portion of which will be locally financed. The reliance on domestic funding for the program's implementation phase is expected to ensure the sustainability of reform initiatives.

## "3. MANAGING THE REFORM PROGRAM

"The effective and efficient management of the reform program is key to its success. Hence, the APJR takes into account the elements essential to good program management. Managing the APJR involves the phasing and programming of the reform projects, the monitoring and evaluation of Program performance, and the establishment and operation of an organic unit that will oversee the entire Program.

"The phasing of specific reform projects in all four sectors looks at the APJR in its entirety in order to keep the attention not only on short-term impacts but also toward more strategic and longer-term approaches to addressing the issues in each sector.

"The development of the proposed sequencing of reform projects aims for the most desirable pace considering the following: (1) which reforms can start independently and simultaneously, (2) which reforms serve as inputs to subsequent reforms, and (3) which are antecedent reforms to other reforms.

"Program monitoring and performance review, on the other hand, are intended to assess the progress and impact of the reform projects, in particular, and the reform program, in general. The results of such review will feed into the improvement of subsequent efforts and pave the way for the continuous reform and improvement of judicial organizations, systems and procedures. The evaluation of completed as well as ongoing projects can also be used [as] feedback [for] future programming and planning activities. The APJR is an evolving document and will continue to benefit from programming and planning processes conducted regularly to ascertain that the Program continues to be responsive to the needs and concerns of the Judiciary and of the entire country.

"The reform program will be managed by an institutional body that will oversee all its activities, including planning, execution, monitoring and evaluation, An inter-agency  steering committee on judicial reform will be constituted to provide policy directions in the implementation of the APJR, while a program management office or change management unit will be institutionalized to handle the technical and administrative aspects of program management. Within the specific implementing or executing offices, there may also be a need for a core group that will be given the responsibility of implementing a specific project."
