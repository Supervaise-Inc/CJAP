# Reforming the Judiciary -- Ch. 13: A Tribute to Valedictorians
Date:       2002-04-06
Publisher:  Supreme Court Printing Press
Source:     Reforming the Judiciary, Supreme Court Printing Press — ch. 13
By: Artemio V. Panganiban

At the outset, let me say that I have nothing but praise and admiration for the Rotary Club of Downtown Manila, led by President Arsenio S. Ong, for sponsoring this gathering - to pay “tribute to the valedictorians” from various high schools in Metropolitan Manila - and for having granted scholarships to many of them to enable them to finish their college education. Indeed, this is one more outstanding demonstration of the Rotary motto “Service above self: he profits most who serves best.”

Let me also congratulate these valedictorians, their parents, school principals and teachers, as well as my fellow Rotarians in this hall who have gathered - 600 strong - to proclaim their faith in and adherence to academic excellence as the key to success in life.

Today’s program reminds me of my own secondary school days some 45 years ago when I graduated from Mapa High School. Like most of you, I came from an impoverished family. My father, a high school graduate, was a poor but honest government employee who worked diligently to provide not only for his four children, but also for his seven brothers and sisters who looked up to him for guidance and assistance after their own father died early on.

To help my parents, I had to hawk newspapers, T shine shoes and peddle cigarettes on the sidewalks of España and Cataluña Streets in Sampaloc, Manila. I also sold bibles to my teachers and textbooks to my classmates, so I could own copies myself as my reward for selling them.

Today, I may have already achieved the dream of every lawyer, which is to be a justice in our Supreme Court. I may have earned enough money to live comfortably in one of the gated villages in Makati and to be able to travel around the world at my leisure. However, I still identify with you. In fact, I am even envious of you because I was not a high school valedictorian. Honor graduate lang po ako, hindi valedictorian. Kaya't hanga po ako sa inyong lahat. Kung nakayanan ko pong magtagumpay sa aking buhay at propesyon, kaya din po ninyong makamit ang myong mga pangarap. Kaya po ba? Kayang-kaya!

Sa pagkakataong ito, bayaan ninyong magbigay ako ng mensahe upang matiyak natin ang maganda ninyong kinabukasan at makamtan din natin ang mga biyayang tinatamasa ng ating mga Rotarians.

Lahat po kayong mga valedictorian ay pinagpala ng ating Maykapal. Binigyan Niya kayo ng katalinuhan at karunungan na di pangkaraniwan. At iyan ang inyong magiging puhunan upang makamtan ninyo ang kaaya ayang kinabukasan.

But academic excellence, by itself, is not enough. While it gives all of you a good beginning or headstart, it must be supplemented by at least three other traits that I believe you must cultivate. These are Patience, Perseverance and Prayer - three Ps.

First, patience. Many times, brilliant individuals become maladjusted in their careers and personal relationships, because they are in so much hurry. They become impetuous, reckless and thoughtless. They run over the rights and the feelings of others. They become too full of themselves and ignore their parents, siblings and friends.

Sometimes, they tend to presume that everyone else is also bright; hence, they do not explain themselves enough. They become overbearing, showy and over-eager. As a result, they turn off people, who regard them as mayabang or mataray.

When I was young like you, I was always in a hurry. And I demanded the same thing from everyone around me. And when they could not measure up, I would get angry. Thus, I had some difficulty relating to other people.

Being too much in a hurry can be likened to overspeeding in driving a vehicle. Recklessness and uncalled-for speed usually end up in accidents and injuries. Remember, patience is a virtue that must be nurtured by the brilliant. Allow enough time for every activity. Be tolerant of contrary opinions. As the saying goes, make haste slowly.

Second, let me discuss perseverance. While you must be patient with other people who may not be as brilliant as you are, at the same time you must be persevering in your own work and activities. Be persistent in your effort. Indeed, persistence and hard work characterize all successful men and women of ages past and present in the Philippines or elsewhere. By this time, you must be familiar with Thomas Edison's motto "Genius is ninety-nine percent perspiration and one percent inspiration." Indeed, Thomas Edison was not satisfied with his bare talent and intellect; he worked long days and nights without ceasing to achieve what he had set out to do.

In more recent times, let me cite Diosdado Banatao, who is probably the most successful Filipino entrepreneur in California. When interviewed by Asiaweek July 21, 2000 issue), he revealed his secret in two words: hard work. He further explained: "For every success, there are 100 or 200 failures." But the success-driven person is not discouraged. He plods on and on. He strives even harder with each failure.

Whatever happens, do not lose heart. Persevere and keep on working. Keep on doing your best. Be reminded of the story of a famous American hero who

* At age 31, failed in business

* At 23 was defeated in a legislative election

* At 53 was overcome by the death of his sweetheart

* At 36 had a nervous breakdown

* At 43, 46 and 48 lost in three successive congressional elections

* At 55 was defeated in a senatorial race

* At 56 failed in his attempt to become Vice President

* At 58 ran for the Senate and lost again

* But at the age of 60 was elected President of the United States.

The man's name is Abraham Lincoln, one of the greatest American presidents.

Truly, there is no substitute for devotion to excellence, no replacement for hard work and high performance, no alternative to believing in ourselves and in our capacity to ride over tragedy and defeat. Some of us lose heart after a few disappointments and failures: a failed subject in school, a frustrated love affair, a lost job, a missed opportunity. Many times, however, adversities and misfortunes are really preludes to greater victory. Let me illustrate that with a little story.

During the Middle Ages, there lived a farmer, his wife, and their only son who was twenty years old. One day the farmer's horse ran away t o the nearby mountains. So, his neighbors went to him and told him, "Bad luck for you, your horse is gone. Now you and your family will starve because you can no longer till the fields.” The farmer replied, “Good luck? Bad luck? Who knows? I will just keep on doing my best." Three days later, the horse returned with nine other wild horses from the mountain. The villagers again trooped to the farmer and said, "Good luck for you. Now you have ten horses." The farmer replied, "Good luck? Bad luck? Who knows? I will just keep trying my best." A few days later, the farmer's only son mounted one of the wild horses which, however, threw him off. Alas, he broke his left leg. Again the villagers told the farmer, "Bad luck for you. With a broken leg, your son is useless in your farm." The farmer replied, "Good luck? Bad luck? Who knows? I will just keep doing my best!" A week later, the kingdom went to war and the king conscripted all the able-bodied young men in the village except, of course, the disabled son of the farmer. The kingdom lost the war, and all the young men from the village died. And all the villagers again visited the farmer and chorused, "Good luck for you. You are the only villager with a son to inherit your farm." And the farmer replied, "Good luck? Bad luck? Who knows? I will just keep doing my best."

Friends, like our farmer, we are besieged by events outside our control. But let us not be frustrated, let us not give up, let us not be overtaken by them. Let us be like the farmer: No matter what happens, whether good or bad, we will continue to do our best, continue to be excellent, continue to persevere in our good work, continue to believe in ourselves, continue to believe in our ultimate victory.

Third, let me discuss the need for prayer. Believe that there is a God who is more powerful than each one of us or all of us combined. He can do what we cannot do; He can make possible what appears to be impossible.

Have faith in God. Many times we pray and ask Him for what we want — for a passing grade, for a good career, for a reciprocated love, for the healing of some illness, or for a job. Sometimes when we do not get what we want, we feel embittered and doubt His power and providence. But our God is an infinitely intelligent and powerful God. He knows our needs better than we do. He will always provide all things good for us — we do. H in His own time and in His own way. As God Himself has said in the Bible, in the book of Isaiah:

My thoughts are not your thoughts,

And my ways are not your ways;

As high as the heavens are above the earth,

So high are my ways above your ways,

And my thoughts above your thoughts.

My dear valedictorians, parents and Rotarians, in all my struggles and trials, God has never abandoned me. Neither will He forget any one of you. I know that for every one who believes and loves Him, God will always be there. Sometimes we may, in our desperation, feel that He has forgotten us because He does not manifest Himself to us, because He does not grant us . exactly what we ask for. But let me assure you that He always answers prayers —in His own way, not our way; in His own time, not at the time we demand. Even in our prayers, we must be patient and persevering.

Ladies and gentlemen, do you know that the Supreme Court and all other courts in the land begin their proceedings with a prayer? So, too, the Senate, the House of Representatives and the Cabinet always start their sessions with a prayer. Our very program today began with an invocation. These are good examples that we must emulate.

Thus, I suggest that we begin each day with a prayer. It may be a memorized one like the "Our Father." Or it may be an original, homemade one. After all, prayer is really our private conversation with God. It is enough to tell Him how much we love and respect Him; it is enough to talk with Him as if we are speaking with a friend. After all, God should really be our best friend. Our language need not be elegant, but it must be sincere and from the heart. It may be in English, Tagalog or any dialect. It may last one minute or one hour. The important thing is that we always remember God daily, dedicate to him whatever we say or do, ask His help in all our undertakings, and petition Him to enable us to solve our problems and difficulties.

Graduates and friends, let me assure you: God watches over all of us and awaits us in eternity. We may not see or hear Him, but He is present here today. He is amongst us, above us to inspire us, before us to lead us, beside us to guide us, behind us to protect us, and within us — in our hearts and our spirits — to possess us and bring us to His everlasting Kingdom.

## Epilogue

Dear valedictorians, let me now summarize my message to you. As you graduate at the top of your class and enter college, you will instantly meet new challenges, difficulties and headaches. But be assured that you will ride over all of them, provided you will remember three words: patience, perseverance and prayer. These are the three Ps that you must combine with your academic excellence to assure yourselves of success and fulfillment.

Some twenty or thirty years from now, while you bask at the summit of your respective careers, it is my hope that in sharing the secret of your success with your children, you will tell them something like this: "I succeeded in my life and career because on April 6, 2002 during a 'Tribute to Valedictorians' sponsored by the Rotary Club of Downtown Manila, I heard, believed, and later on practiced patience, perseverance and prayer — the three Ps - that our guest speaker, Justice Panganiban, spoke about." By that time, I would most probably be in another life, but I would still be proudly watching over you, perfectly content that my 20-minute speech today has borne fruit. Maraming salamat po.
