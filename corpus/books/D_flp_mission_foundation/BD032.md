# Leadership by Example: The Davide Standard -- Ch. 2: A Friendly Court

Many court watchers have compared the Supreme Court to an unsolvable puzzle which is contained in a riddle shrouded in mystery and imbedded in an enigma. Although they may appreciate its role as the protector of the Constitution and the defender of the peoples’ liberties, they find it difficult to understand its mystic ways and traditions. Oftentimes, they look at it as a collection of grumpy old men with failing eyesights, whose pictures they cannot scan on their TV sets or recognize in their newspapers. Although Supreme Court decisions deeply affect their lives and their fortunes, people find magistrates so low-profile that they cannot be picked out in a tapestry of common mortals. But ordinary mortals they definitely are not, because their decisions speak of precedence and continuity bordering on eternity, as if the writers have been with the Court since its birth almost a century ago.

Even to lawyers, the Court may appear distant, impregnable and impersonal. Ninety-nine percent of the time, the Court is merely an abstraction, much like a deity on Mt. Olympus, to which they file petitions, supplications and prayers; and from which, after the lapse of an unpredictable period, promulgations and commandments come forth. Alas, in one percent of the time, the Court comes out of its hidden fortress during oral arguments, but only to further mystify them with hostile and perplexing questions, sometimes to their embarrassment, in the presence of their valued clients.

But once in a while, the Court displays its noncombative human face, even a happy one, as its members don not just their dull all-black working robes, but their scarlet-lined ceremonial togas during ceremonies to honor their retiring colleagues. While still perched on their lonely crescent-shaped elevated rostrum — which, if you will note, has no front exit — justices discard their impatient frowns, so typical during oral arguments, and smile and display the demure face of the Tribunal. They not only nod and wave little fingers in obvious recognition of friendly faces in the crowd, but they even come down from their Olympian thrones and speak before the microphones normally reserved for mere lawyers.

And so the ceremonies today must be a welcome respite to all of you, our dear audience. While unusual because they are meant to honor the Chief, they are traditional, because they are all done in familiar numbered sequences culled from a century of usage. Indeed, this afternoon the Court is not just human. It is uncharacteristically friendly. And it has all the reasons in the world to be, because it is honoring its leader and proclaiming not only his virtues but also the collective achievements of the Court which he ably headed 1 during the last seven years.

Mga pinagpipitagang panauhin at kaibigan, tuloy po kayo at makiisa sa aming maligayang pagbubunyi sa ating Punong Mahistrado.

I ask all of you present today to sit easy, relax, and smile as the program moves on. Come to think of it, this is one time in this rather intimidating and cavernous hall when you can even put your hands together and clap. Thank you.

## Summary

Chapter 2 of 'Leadership by Example: The Davide Standard' is Panganiban's brief welcome at the ceremonies honoring the retiring Chief Justice, and it turns on a single contrast. Court watchers, he observes, have compared the Supreme Court to an unsolvable puzzle contained in a riddle shrouded in mystery and imbedded in an enigma: they may appreciate its role as protector of the Constitution and defender of the people's liberties, but they find its mystic ways impenetrable and often picture its members as a collection of grumpy old men with failing eyesights, so low-profile that they cannot be picked out in a tapestry of common mortals. Yet ordinary mortals they are not, he says, because their decisions speak of precedence and continuity bordering on eternity, as though their authors had been with the Court since its birth almost a century earlier. Even to lawyers the Court is distant, impregnable and impersonal, an abstraction on Mt. Olympus that receives petitions, supplications and prayers and, after an unpredictable lapse, issues promulgations and commandments; in the one percent of the time it emerges from its hidden fortress for oral arguments, it mystifies counsel with hostile and perplexing questions before their valued clients. Retirement ceremonies are the exception. The justices exchange their dull all-black working robes for scarlet-lined ceremonial togas, discard their impatient frowns, nod and wave in recognition of friendly faces, and come down from their Olympian thrones to speak at microphones normally reserved for mere lawyers. The day's proceedings, he adds, are unusual because they honor the Chief himself but traditional because they follow familiar numbered sequences culled from a century of usage, and the Court has every reason to be friendly because it is proclaiming both its leader's virtues and the collective achievements of the seven years he headed it. He welcomes the guests in Tagalog and closes by inviting them to sit easy, relax, smile, and for once even applaud.
