# Chapter 9: Bukas Loob sa Diyos: Called to Evangelize

(Editor's note: This is a stirring reflection delivered before a prayer-meeting of the Bukas Loob Sa Diyos Catholic Covenant Community (BLD] held on February 23, 1994 at the Philippine International Convention Center. The author is a member of the Council of Servant Leaders [roughly equivalent to the board of trustees) of BLD and chairman of the BLD Foundation. The BLD is a union of "renewed and encountered" couples, families and individuals.)

The reign of God is at hand. Reform your lives and believe in the gospel.

Mark 1:15

Brothers and sisters in Christ, let me tell you the story of a young man whom we shall call Pancracio. He was welleducated, talented and hard-working and he devoted all his waking hours to work. Soon enough, his business prospered, his stature grew and his reputation spread. At 35 years, Pancracio became a multi-millionaire. One day, while driving his brand-new BMW, he met an accident and was rushed to Makati Medical Center. While in a coma at the ICU, he had a vision of death. Mr. Death, in his sinister black robe, knocked at his hospital door and demanded his life. "Mr. Death," Pancracio pleaded, "please do not take me now. I am still young and I have barely begun my life. Please give me a chance to enjoy the wealth I have worked so hard for. Besides, I am not yet ready. In my rush to provide for my material enjoyment, I neglected God. I never prayed. I never minded my neighbor. I do not even know the Commandments. I will

surely go to hell! Please give me another chance."

Mr. Death was considerate with him. He agreed not to take him at that time. And in a moment of generosity, he added that the next time, he would not come during an emergency. He agreed to give our hero a few warnings before his next visit.

And so Pancracio recovered from his near-fatal injuries and became whole again. And because of his vision, he developed a healthy fear of God. He brought his wife to the Marriage Encounter (ME) and dutifully took the Life in the Spirit Seminar (LSS). A few years passed and alas, Pancracio was again distracted from the straight and narrow path. Profligate and weak in spirit, he soon became attracted once more to wealth, women and wine. He forgot his Scripture readings, neglected his prayers and reflections, and stopped attending prayer meetings. At age 45, he wallowed in money, power and fame, and sported the ultimate symbol of success - a beautiful mistress and a young girl friend besides.

One night, after an exciting evening of merry making, his heart failed him and he was brought once more to Makati Medical. And once more, Mr. Death, in his sinister black robe, came knocking at his door. But it was not going to be easy. Pancracio once more protested. "But you promised not to take me during an emergency. You promised to give me sufficient warning. Why are you here now so suddenly?"

Replied Mr. Death in a monotone: "I gave you sufficient warning. You had to wear eyeglasses because I diminished your eyesight. You had yourself fitted with new dentures because I loosened a few of your teeth. Some years ago, you had a medical checkup and you were told that your uric acid and cholesterol were sky-flying and that your blood sugar exceeded 300. How can you complain to me now that I did not give you sufficient warning?"

Brothers and sisters, in our lives there is a part of Pancracio who lurks inside us, mischievous, procrastinating, rationalizing and

sinful. But more than the medical bulletins that our doctors give us is the direct warning of our Lord as He exhorts us today: "The reign of God is at hand! Reform your lives and believe in the Gospel." When that fateful encounter inevitably comes, we cannot complain that our God did not forewarn us. In the Gospel reading today, He directs us to "reform your lives and believe in the Gospel." That is more than a warning. It is an order.

On yet another occasion, our Lord was more specific and blunt: "As to the exact day or hour, no one knows it, neither the angels in heaven nor the Son, but only the Father. Be constantly on the watch! Stay awake! You do not know when the appointed time will come." (Mark 13:32-34).

The Three Cs of Reformation Tonight, brothers and sisters, this reflection will be centered on the call of our Lord for a reformation of our lives - and I would like to give you three ideas to ponder during this Lenten season: first, REPENTANCE or CONTRITION; second, TRANS- FORMATION or CONVERSION; and third, EVANGELIZA- TION Or CHRISTIANIZATION. Let me say those three words again: repentance, transformation and evangelization. For those who like memory aids, let us use the equivalent three "Cs": CONTRITION, CONVERSION and CHRISTIANIZATION. Contrition for our sins should bring us to conversion to the ways of Jesus, which in turn should lead us to the true Christianization or evangelization of the people around us. These three ideas however do not necessarily constitute graduated stages that must come one after the other. A spiritually mature person may already be evangelizing but may still practise repentance and could be in various phases of transformation.

Repentance or Contrition To repent or to be contrite means to be sincerely sorry for our sins; to admit our faults and to be man enough to face the consequences thereof. Our God is not a vengeful God. He is a forgiving God. He forgives us and erases our transgressions once we confess our sins and truly feel sorry for them. Our Church tells us that the only way to gain total forgiveness of serious wrongdoing or mortal sin is through the sacrament of reconciliation and absolution given by a priest. Hence, BLD members are encouraged to confess and obtain absolution once a month, because "no one knows the exact day or hour."

For all of us in BLD who have been renewed in the spirit through a personal encounter with Jesus Christ, repentance is a daily part of our lives. It is a part of our daily prayer. It is a part of our evening reflections before going to bed. It is a part of our on-going all-day conversations with the Lord. Repentance cleanses us not only spiritually but also physically and psychologically. We know that without true repentance, our prayer is without power. The healing grace of the Lord is blocked by our hard-heartedness, sinfulness and resentments which can be expunged from our system only by sincere contrition.

Real repentance or contrition means not only being sorry for the consequences of our sins. Rather, it means that we hate sin itself. We are sorry for our transgressions not only because like Pancracio, we are afraid of the fury of hell but more so because we seek the comforting love of God. Sin is the opposite of God. And he who loves God hates sin. Some people stop at a red light because they are afraid of being caught by a policeman; others follow the light because they believe that is the correct thing to do: because they believe in orderly traffic. Repentance is not just the fear of being caught. Repentance is hatred for the crime and faith in the righteousness of Christ.

Contrition transcends mere thinking and talking. It is not

enough to say, "I am sorry." It is also necessary to restitute what has been taken or to repair what has been damaged. Hence, a thief must return the object taken and a person who injures another must pay for the medical expenses of the injured. The Scriptures tell us that after his encounter with the Lord, Zacchaeus, the chief tax collector of the Jews, gave half of his belongings to the poor and paid back four-fold everyone he had defrauded. In response, the Lord joyfully said to him: "Today, salvation has come to this house for this is what it means to be a son of Abraham." (Luke 19:9)

Transformation or Conversion As we develop and practice repentance and restitution, conversion necessarily follows. Conversion is a firm commitment never to sin again and more than that, it is a vow to follow Jesus in words, thoughts and actions.

It is a "call to a change of heart, to metanoia, to a profound transformation of one's way of life so that it would be in accord with the gospel." It is speaking with Jesus' words, loving with His love and living with His life. Conversion often takes a whole lifetime; it is a day-to-day grinding, grasping and following of Christ. Normally, it starts in childhood catechism, through high school and college apologetics. Sometimes however, we hear of instant conversions, especially among charismatics and marriage encounter graduates. Almost instantly, by God's miracle, a sinner suddenly repents, a prodigal son returns, a philandering husband reforms. This is not really unusual. Such was the case of St. Paul on that historic road to Damascus. Of St. Augustine who turned his back on worldly pleasure. And of the repentant thief to whom the gentle Jesus, gasping for His last words, said, "I assure you, this day you will be with me in paradise." (Luke 23:43).

Conversion means not only following the Ten Commandments, but living the positive gospel of Jesus starting from the beatitudes and the parables then to experiencing in our own lives the passion, death and resurrection of our Lord.

Evangelization or Christianization Once we are converted, we begin to glow. People begin to notice us. People begin to see the change in our lifestyles, in our language, in our relationships, in our actions, in our habits. Unconsciously we become witnesses of Christ. We become evangelizers. Our transformation becomes a shining lamp that cannot be covered and hidden in the night. As our Lord exhorted His disciples during His sermon on the Mount: "You are the light of the world. A city set on a hill cannot be hidden. Men do not light a lamp and then put it under a bushel basket. They set it on a stand where it gives light to all in the house. In the same way, your light must shine before men so that they may see goodness in your acts and give praise to your heavenly Father." (Matt. 5:14-16).

We cannot go to heaven alone. We need to bring our family, our friends and our neighbors. We are called to promote human development. We are tasked to break the yoke of the poor, fight injustice, liberate man from bigotry and work for peace. The evangelist Matthew records that during the final judgment, God will not ask us how many digits there were in our bankbook, or how many Mercedeses and BMWs we owned, or how many PhDs we garnered, or how high our position was in our government. He will ask what we did for the least of our brethren: "Did you feed the hungry... clothe the naked... shelter the homeless.. heal the sick.

and comfort the prisoners?" (cf. Matt. 25:31-46).

At that moment of judgment, the lyrics of an anointed song which we sing during our prayer meetings will consume us: "We fought the fight, we ran the race. Then at last we'll see Jesus face to face. Our eyes will clear, we'll fin'lly see that it's only what we've done for Christ that will last eternally."

The crowning glory of conversion is our relentless pursuit of evangelization. The fulfillment of evangelization is when the evangelized becomes the evangelizer himself; when the student

becomes the teacher; when the lamb becomes the shepherd. To paraphrase Santa Teresa of Avila, the evangelized become Christ to others, so others may be Christ to still many others.

Evangelization or Christianization by the renewed faithful is the battle cry of the Second Plenary Council of the Philippines. In the words of PCP II, and I quote: "Evangelization must also seek to build up the Church, so that it becomes a people made one with the unity of the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit, a community of Christ's disciples, a visible mystery of communion and mission, and the sacrament, in Christ, of universal salvation both signifying and serving the union of persons with God, and the unity of all humankind. (190)

"Evangelization does not stop at the building of the Church. It seeks to transform the whole fabric of society according to the values of the Kingdom and of Christ. That is why the Church has not hesitated to promote total human development, integral liberation, justice and peace in society and the integrity of creation. In the Philippines, this facet of renewed evangelization needs to be emphasized. For while the majority of our people are Catholics and our churches are filled on Sundays, our society remains a sick society." (192) The Transformation of BLD Brothers and sisters, as I explained earlier, a person who is truly repentant undergoes radical conversion or transformation. And a transformed Christian soon becomes an enthusiastic evangelizer. This is the recurring process that the men and women of God undergo day after day. This is the same process that our Community has undergone and continues to undergo now.

The BLD was founded 10 years ago. It started with the encounters: the Marriage Encounter, then the Life in the Spirit Seminar and later the Family Encounter. Then, we developed our

growth and discipleship programs. For many years, the BLD was engaged in the apostolate of repentance and radical conversion for its members. Two years ago, the BLD started to look outward to the larger Church and to join the Church's renewed evangelization program.

The "signs of the times" for BLD started when four BLD members - Sonny de los Reyes, Robert Corpus, Pat Menzon and myself - were conscripted to join the Second Plenary Council of the Philippines in early 1991. This is significant because no other lay organization, whether diocesan or national, had an equal number of lay members in that historic council. To me, this conscription was the first great signal of our Lord in calling on the BLD and its members to help transform not only themselves but to evangelize the larger Church as well. The second great "sign of the times" was Sonny's election in 1991 and reelection in 1993 as president of the Council of the Laity of the Philippines. By his elevation, Sonny became head not only of BLD, not only of the federation of charismatic communities, but of all Catholic lay organizations - both national and diocesan. The final unmistakable great sign of our Lord is the emergence of the BLD's new vision-charism-mission that so clearly answered God's call for renewed evangelization of the family, of basic ecclesial communities and of the parishes of our country as well as for breaking the centuries-old yoke of poverty and oppression and finally, for raising an army of prayer warriors.

In addition to these three great "signs of the times," there is one more that I think the Lord especially addressed to me personally. You see, I sincerely believe that I do not deserve, as in fact, I did not expect and I did not want to be a Servant Leader of our Community. Two years prior the emergence process, Leni and I decided to be just ordinary members of our Community. During those two years, we did not lead a prayer meeting, we declined as sharers in our MEs and LSS, did not act as teachers and went on leave as stewards of the Community. Just as our Lord Jesus Christ 2,000 years ago exhorted His disciples, today He addresses the same message to all of us who are gathered here, which I quote as follows: "Go, therefore, make disciples of all nations. Lord's call was so pervading and so deafening that all I could really answer, after all the running away, was "Here I am Lord." And so, here I am brothers and sisters, ready to do God's work in our Community and in our Church.

The Call to Christianize This call for renewed evangelization is timely. Consider the statistics. A majority of parish priests minister to about 40,000 to 50,000 parishioners on the average. Our priests may be supermen but pastoring 40,000 parishioners is simply impossible without the active participation of renewed lay evangelizers. Studies submitted to the Catholic Bishops' Conference of the Philippines further show that only 2 percent of all Catholics are active in church-related affairs, and only 10 percent are considered regular churchgoers or "stockholders" of the Church. Hence, of the 56 million Filipino Catholics, only about a million are active and only five million are regular churchgoers. Of those who partake of the sacraments, most are women, the "highly audible-visible laity." Majority of men may be termed as the "reluctant laity." Approximately 88 percent, or about 50 million, are the "invisible laity" - unchurched, uninvolved and untouched by the parish. Small wonder, we hear so often the cacophonous and almost blasphemous boast of many: "I am a Catholic but I do not believe in the majesterium of the church." What a sad contradiction.

These 88 percent who are unchurched, uninvolved and untouched by the parish pose the great challenge to all of us today. Itis obvious that the vast majority of our people need to repent, to be re-converted and to be re-evangelized. They need to be touched by God's saving and liberating Word. We in this hall, the chosen ones of God, whom the Scripture calls "His royal priesthood," are called upon to herald Jesus' call for a massive reformation of hearts and minds.
