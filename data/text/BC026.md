# Love God, Serve Man -- Ch. 8: Why is the Catholic Church in Politics?
Date:       1992-03-11
Publisher:  Philippine Daily Inquirer (ed. Isagani Yambot)
Source:     Love God, Serve Man, Philippine Daily Inquirer (ed. Isagani Yambot) — ch. 8 (batch-04 split)
By: Artemio V. Panganiban

(Editor's note: During the 1992 national elections, the Catholic Church decided to sponsor the formation of the Parish Pastoral Council for Responsible Voting [PPCRV] for the purpose of assuring peaceful, honest, orderly and credible elections. The PPCRV fielded more than 300,000 volunteers in almost all precincts all over the country. The Commission on Elections accredited PPCRV as its citizens' arm and later credited it with a major role in the peaceful holding of the election. This article is a comprehensive summary of several speeches that Atty. Artemio V. Panganiban, 'who was [and still is]PPCRV national vice chairman and chief legal counsel, delivered in many fora and meetings all over the country. This article was published in full on the front page of the Philippine Daily Inquirer on March 11, 1992 and in the Starweek, weekly magazine of the Philippine Star on April 26, 1992. It seeks to justify the Catholic Church's participation in the political, albeit non-partisan, life of the country.)

Many people are asking: "Why is the Catholic Church getting involved in politics? Politics is dirty. Does the Church want to be smeared by its mud? Is this political involvement not a violation of the revered separation of Church and State?"

I will try to answer these questions and explain the surging phenomenon of Church involvement in political affairs. More specifically, (1) WHY was the Church's new political arm - the Parish Pastoral Council For Responsible Voting (PPCRV) - organized? (2) WHAT is the PPCRV in the first place? and (3) HOW will the PPCRV achieve its goals?

Why the PPCRV Prior to 1965, Church involvement in politics was largely unheard of. Since then, however, the Philippine Church has increasingly been visible in the political sphere. During the peaceful revolution of 1986 which saw the dismantling of the Marcos government and during the snap election just before that, the Philippine Church represented by Catholic Bishops' Conference of the Philippines (CBCP) assumed a pro-active role in a "Joint Pastoral Exhortation on the Snap Election" dated January 25, 1986: The CBCP through its then president, Ricardo Cardinal Vidal, exhorted the faithful to "vote into office the persons you believe God wants you to put into office as President and Vice President of this land" and "to exercise unwavering vigilance in organized collaboration with others so that your votes are cast, counted, tallied and reported properly and honestly." More strongly, in a Post-election Statement, the CBCP condemned the snap election as "unparalleled" in its fraud attested by "systematic disenfranchisement of voters... widespread and massive vote-buying... deliberate tampering with the election returns... and intimidation, harassment, terrorism and murder."

For the forthcoming 1992 election, the Church has become even more involved in political activities. Whereas in 1986, the CBCP merely endorsed the effort of NAMFREL which was a civic organization, in this election the Church directly sponsors the PPCRV. To be sure, the PPCRV was created out of a confluence of three major events: (1) the Second Vatican Council (1962-1965), (2) the Second Plenary Council of the Philippines (Jan. 20-Feb. 17,

1991) and (3) three pastoral letters issued recently by the CBCP.

## The Second Vatican Council (1962-1965)

The Second Vatican Council which was convened in 1962 and adjourned in 1965, produced 16 documents governing and

transforming many aspects of Christian life. Vatican II, as it is commonly called, underscored the need for man's total or integral development. Simply put, this means that a Christian should act out his faith in all aspects of his life, whether spiritual, temporal, economic, social, cultural or political. In other words, a Christian's integral development requires him to apply gospel values wherever he is and whatever he does, whether at home, in school, in the office, in the polling place or in public office.

Hence, the Church must be wherever there is sinfulness, persecution, injustice and violation of human dignity. It is concerned not merely with the "life hereafter" but also with the temporal life, here and now. Christianity means not merely salvation of souls for the next life but the liberation of man in the present life from all forms of oppression so that he may fully develop in all dimensions - spiritual, economic, social, cultural and political.

Vatican II also changed the meaning of "church." Formerly, "church" referred to the hierarchy. But Vatican II says that "church" is composed not only of the hierarchy and the religious but also of the laity. It gave lay people new initiatives and new responsibilities.

Furthermore, the laity's equality with the clergy "in dignity and mission" was expressly recognized by Vatican II. Hence, lay organizations no longer need a "mandate" from the local ordinary. Now lay people may pursue their Christian life and mission without need of a specific "mandate" other than their baptismal empowerment.

Finally, Christifideles Laici (42) stresses that the lay faithful are "never to relinquish participation in public life." They are not to regard politics or public life as something to be shunned. On the contrary, good Christians, if they are qualified, should be encouraged to be in public life, to "restore all things in Christ."

The Second Plenary Council of the Philippines (1991)

Echoing the teachings of Vatican II, the Second Plenary Council of the Philippines (PCP II), which was held from January 20 to February 17, 1991, gave the following theological direction: "In the Philippines today, given the moral perception that politics has become an obstacle to integral development, the urgent necessity is for the lay faithful to participate more actively, with singular competence and integrity in political affairs." (PCP II, p.63)

Specific legal sanction for political involvement is provided by PCP II Resolution 28 which states:

1."Lay men and women in responsible positions in our society must help form the civic conscience of the voting population and work to explicitly promote the election to public office of leaders of true integrity.

2."Bishops, priests and religious, commonly identified as 'the Church,' must refrain from partisan politics, avoiding especially the use of the pulpit for partisan purposes to avoid division among the flock they shepherd." Please note that under Paragraph 1 of Resolution 28, lay people are given two responsibilities; first, that they must "help form the civic conscience of the voting population" and, second, that they should "work to explicitly promote the election to public office of leaders of true integrity."

CBCP Pastoral Letters Taking off from the theological direction of PCP-II, the CBCP recently issued three pastoral letters in connection with the

1992 general elections.

The first of these was issued on July 22, 1991. It challenged the laity to see to it that three "indispensable requirements are met so that the elections in 1992 and in subsequent years may serve as democratic instruments for establishing and changing political leaders and for securing social changes beneficial to many people." These are:

1. "The first requirement is a wise, informed and formed electorate... who will reject as objectionable and harmful our typical politics of pay-offs, patronage and personalities;

2. "The second requirement is the existence of conditions that will enable voters to choose freely... where the electorate will reject the politics of) guns, goons and gold.

3. "The third requirement is the organization of our people down to the precinct level to combat the age-old practices calculated to falsify the expressed wishes of the people and to attain victory at any cost...like) ballot box stuffing, switching and falsification of election documents and the intimidation and bribery of election officials." In response to this CBCP pastoral letter, the Parish Pastoral Council For Responsible Voting (PPCRV) was organized under the leadership of well-known lay leader Tita de Villa in August 1991 and formally launched in the Archdiocese of Manila on October 19,

1991. To the call of our bishops to eliminate the politics of payoffs, patronage and personalities, the PPCRV responded with a thunderous "YES, we will reject these three Ps and in their stead, we will uphold the Ps of PRINCIPLES, PLATFORMS AND PER- FORMANCE." Furthermore, PPCRV added, "we shall pray, pray

and pray unceasingly."

To the call to remove guns, goons and gold, PPCRV says, "YES, we will reject these 3 Gs and in their place, we shall proclaim the glory, grace and gospel of God." And to the challenge to organize our people, the PPCRV says, "YES, we shall poll-watch and we shall organize our parishes down to the precinct level and fight fraudulent ways of prostituting the election process."

On November 28, 1991, the CBCP issued a second pastoral letter entitled "Renewing the Political Order," which outlined guidelines on choosing candidates for the 1992 elections. The CBCP said that the faithful must look for candidates who are Maka- Diyos, Maka-Tao and Maka-Bayan. In response to this pastoral letter, the PPCRV issued its "Qualification Standards for Candidates for Public Service." These are a set of detailed guidelines to enable voters to choose their candidates meaningfully.

Not satisfied with issuing two letters, the CBCP on January 31, 1992 issued a third. The Philippine Bishops said that the major danger in the election is not really violence or force but the manipulative use of money; in other words, the politics of payoffs.

In response, the PPCRV has called for a rejection of all candidates who bribe voters or indulge in too much expenditures and campaign gimmicks. It is also asking voters to discriminate in favor of candidates who have no vested financial interests. While it is relatively easier to spot candidates who use goons and terrorism, it is relatively more difficult to fight, not to say prosecute, candidates who use excessive "gold" as the means to get elected. In the politics of payoffs, the giver and the receiver do not announce their unholy transactions. And in a country where the majority of the voters are poor, money politics is truly a monstrous temptation.

## What is the PPCRV?

Now that we know the rationale, more accurately, the theology of Church involvement in politics (the WHY of PPCRV), let me discuss the WHAT of this new Church organization.

FIRST, let me define it comprehensively, as a lawyer would:

The Parish Pastoral Council for Responsible Voting is a

national, parish-based, political but non-partisan lay movement supported by the hierarchy, whose immediate objective is the responsible use of the right to vote and whose ultimate goal is the promotion of the common welfare and the fostering of good government and true democracy through an educational program based on gospel values and Church teachings and whose special target is the youth and first-time voters.

Now let us examine this definition part by part.

PPCRV. is a National, Parish-based, Non-partisan Lay Movement PPCRV is national: it covers 2,300 parishes in 78 dioceses all over the Philippines. In Metro Manila, the aim is to be able to recruit at least 2,000 members per parish. For parishes outside the Archdiocese of Manila, the goal is to list down a minimum of 1,000 members per parish. Assuming an average of only 1,000 members in each parish, the national movement will command a membership of at least 2.3 million by election time.

In a unanimous en banc resolution dated February 18, 1992, the Commission on Elections accredited the PPCRV as Comelec's national citizen's arm in the 1992 election.

The PPCRV is parish-based: it makes use of the existing Church structure.

The Parish Council is usually made up of representatives of all church organizations in the parish. The existing council in every parish is asked to form the PPCRV unit for its locality, under the guidance of the parish priest. The PPCRV's strength is in the parish. In this sense, the PPCRV is the only massbased electoral movement that reaches directly the precincts.

The PPCRV, while political, is non-partisan: it will not endorse candidates or political parties. Hence, members cannot campaign or even publicly announce their political choices. Otherwise, they will be considered resigned from the movement.

The PPCRV is a lay movement. It is led by the laity. Hence, the national officials are all lay people. And so with diocesan and parish officers.

The PPCRV is supported by the hierarchy who may take on roles as advisers. Hence, PPCRV-National has a five-man Board of Advisers composed of Bishop Carmelo Morelos (CBCP president), Archbishop Leonardo Legaspi (immediate past president of CBCP), Archbishop Orlando Quevedo, Bishop Angel Lagdameo and Bishop Teodoro Bacani Jr. with H.E. Jaime Cardinal Sin and

H.E. Ricardo Cardinal Vidal as honorary co-chairmen.

PPCRV Stands for Voter Education and Poll-watching The PPCRV's immediate objective is to educate people in the responsible use of the right to vote. We urge them to be guided by their conscience. The ultimate goal is the promotion of the common welfare, good government and democracy.

The PPCRV's basic thrust is educational. At present, the PPCRV has five seminar modules, all dealing with different aspects of voter education. Secondarily, the PPCRV will also pool-watch

and assist other organizations, like Media Quick Count, in their electoral functions.

Since 60 percent of the voting population is below age 30, there will be special emphasis on the youth and first-time voters. The PPCRV has a youth arm which will be organizing activities designed for the youth of the parish.

## How Will PPCRV Achieve Its Goals?

What activities will the PPCRV undertake to achieve its goals of (1) "consciencitizing" the voter, (2) poll-watching and, (3) assisting "quick count" organizations?

1. On the national level, the PPCRV is conducting a trimedia (TV, radio and print) educational campaign. It has solicited P10 million worth of advertising in the form of air time and print space from three big companies: PLDT, San Miguel and Meralco. Guidelines for Electing Public Officials

2. As adverted to earlier, the PPCRV has also come out with "Qualification Standards for Candidates for Public Office." These consist of two major parts: (1) guidelines for disqualification and (2) guidelines for qualification. The first disqualifies those candidate denounced by the CBCP pastoral letters, namely those who employ any of the 3 Ps (payoff, patronage and personalities) or the 3 Gs (guns, goons and gold) or who have a record or reputation for dishonesty and unexplained wealth. The second helps voters identify those who are most deserving. They are divided into three general categories: (1) personal attributes, (2) platform and (3) track record. Hence, voters

are urged to examine (1) what a candidate is, (2) what he stands for and (3) what he has done. The guidelines are quite detailed and are the subject of a separate education module.

The question is often asked: Under the guidelines, may a Catholic vote for a non-Catholic candidate? The answer is a clear yes. All that we require is that the candidate must practise his or her faith. If Christian, he must live gospel values and if Muslim, he must follow the Koran faithfully.

Bishop Teodoro Bacani Jr., in his recent book "Church in Politics," explains the reasons for this: "Up to now, no non-Catholic aspirant to the presidency has publicly proclaimed a position which the Catholic Church cannot swallow or would be hard put to live with. And in case a non-Catholic is elected president, it would probably be more difficult for him to adjust to such a new situation than for the Church and its officials to adjust to a non-Catholic president." (Pp.69-70)

3. The PPCRV-National has also formed a Research Committee to look into the background, attributes, platform and track record of national candidates and will make known its findings soon. These findings will be objective and non-partisan and may be used by voters in conjunction with the "Qualification Standards."

4. The PPCRV also publishes and distributes various publications like Bishop Bacani's "Church in Politics," Msgr. Bayani Valenzuela's "Katesismo Sa Pagboto," the "Ten Commandments of Voting," the "Voter's Prayer" (which is usually prayed after the Mass), etc.

## Parish-level Activities

5. On the parish level, the following activities are suggested: the holding of educational and candidates' fora for the purpose of educating the voters on the electoral procedures and of

informing them about the platforms and track record of the local candidates; the distribution of educational materials; sign-up campaigns; prayer/fasting rallies; and formation of legal, education and research committees. Other practical suggestions are: encouraging voters to vote early, to prepare their candidates' list ahead of election day and to poll-watch.

## Conclusion

The PPCRV is a mass-based non-partisan movement of at least 2.3 million voters. It aims to preserve democracy and achieve not only free and orderly polls but also meaningful elections where only the deserving are elected. It will be around not only for the 1992 elections, but in every election which will be held every three years thereafter. The PPCRV aims to clean up the dirty world of politics and to uphold Christian values. In short, the PPCRV is a "faith response" to the challenge to free our people from the oppression and sinfulness of politics.

Seen in this light, the PPCRV as the Church arm in political action cannot be violating the principle of separation of Church and State. In the first place, although priests and bishops act as advisers, the PPCRV is led by and is composed of lay people. In the second place, the PPCRV is non-partisan. It does not endorse candidates or political parties. And in the third place, no constitutional provision or statute is violated by lay people who form non-partisan electoral movements, even where their advisers are members of the clergy.

The PPCRV does not encourage the men of the cloth to run for public office. In the words of PCP II this "would be divisive of the flock they shepherd." But I daresay that even if they do, they would not be violating the Constitution.

One last point: the 1992 election is being contested by eight presidential candidates. Even if the number is eventually reduced,

still the expectation of many is that the new president will be elected by only a plurality - perhaps as little as 30 percent of the electorate. If the elections are tainted by fraud and the other candidates refuse to concede, the country may be rocked by discontent, strife and instability. The Comelec valiantly says this will not happen, and the PPCRV will assist Comelec in its valiant work. But, in the remote event it does happen, the PPCRV, with its 2.3 million non-partisan and freedom-loving members, could provide the rock upon which the state may build its foundation. The PPCRV will provide the stabilizing and moderating force so essential to the growth and development of our country. The PPCRV will be there to defend our democracy and our freedom - before, during and after the elections.

I thank you.
